## Recognising Abuse

*Child abuse is any act that harms a child's health, survival, development or dignity. Under Kenyan law, children have the right to be protected from all forms of abuse. **Abuse is NEVER the child's fault** — no matter what an abuser says.*

| Type of Abuse | What It Includes | Signs to Watch For |
|--------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **1. Physical Abuse** | Hitting, beating, shaking, burning or any physical harm inflicted on a child | Unexplained bruises, burns or fractures; fear of going home; flinching when touched |
| **2. Sexual Abuse** | Any sexual act involving a child: touching private parts; making a child touch someone else's private parts; showing pornography; taking sexual photos; penetration (defilement under Kenyan law) | Difficulty walking or sitting; bleeding/pain/discharge from private parts; knowledge of sexual acts beyond their age; regressive behaviours (bedwetting, thumb-sucking) |
| **3. Emotional/Psychological Abuse** | Constant criticism, humiliation, rejection, threats; withholding love; witnessing domestic violence | Withdrawn, anxious or depressed; extreme behaviour (very aggressive or very passive); delayed development |
| **4. Neglect** | Failure to provide basic needs: food, shelter, clothing, medical care, education; abandonment | Constant hunger, poor hygiene, untreated medical needs, frequently absent from school |
| **5. Nutritional Abuse** | Withholding food or failing to provide adequate nutrition. A study at Moi Teaching & Referral Hospital found this was the most common form of abuse seen, affecting 39% of abused children. | Stunted growth, extreme thinness, signs of malnutrition |
| **6. Child Labour & Exploitation** | Forcing a child to work in hazardous conditions; trafficking for labour or sex; **early marriage (child marriage)** | Missing school frequently; signs of exhaustion; adult relationships with an older person who provides gifts |

⚠️ **Kenya Law — Penalties for Defilement (Sex with Child Under 18)**

- Child aged **1–11 years:** Possible **life imprisonment**
- Child aged **12–15 years:** Not less than **20 years imprisonment**
- Child aged **16–17 years:** Not less than **15 years imprisonment**

## Reporting Pathways in Kenya

*If you or someone you know has experienced abuse, reporting is essential. Kenya has multiple reporting pathways designed to protect survivors and bring perpetrators to justice. Reporting can feel scary, but you do not have to go through it alone.*

| Where | What They Do | Contact / Note |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| **Police Station** | Take your statement; issue P3 form for medical examination; connect you to Gender Desk or Child Protection Unit | Ask specifically for the **Gender Police Officer** — specially trained for SGBV/child cases |
| **Any Public Hospital** | Medical examination and treatment; evidence collection (within 72–96 hrs of assault); PEP for HIV (must start within 72 hrs); emergency contraception; counselling | Go even if you are unsure — medical care is critical. If possible, do not bathe or change clothes first (preserves evidence) |
| **Department of Children's Services** | Intervene, provide temporary shelter, assist with legal processes | www.childrenservices.go.ke · Sub-County Children's Officer in your area |
| **Schools** | Head teacher, guidance counsellor or class teacher. Schools have child protection policies and are required to report abuse. | You can ask a trusted teacher to accompany you to report |

**Emergency Contacts**

- **116** — Child Helpline Kenya (Free · 24 hours)
- **1195** — GBV Hotline (Free · 24 hours)
- **112** — Police Emergency (24 hours)

## What Happens After Reporting

1. **Statement taking** — Tell your story to trained officers
2. **Medical examination** — At hospital (bring P3 form from police)
3. **Evidence collection** — If within 72–96 hours of assault
4. **Protection** — Witness protection available if you face threats
5. **Investigation** — Police gather evidence
6. **Prosecution** — Perpetrator is charged in court
7. **Support** — Counselling and psychosocial support throughout

### If You've Been Sexually Assaulted

- **Do not bathe or change clothes** if possible — evidence is critical
- Go to a hospital **immediately**
- **PEP for HIV** must be started within 72 hours — do not delay
- Get emergency contraception if relevant
- Report to police — you can be accompanied by a trusted adult

**You are not to blame. Nothing you did caused this.**

💡 **Safe Houses & Rescue Centres**

County governments — such as Kakamega — have established safe houses and rescue centres where survivors can be temporarily hosted for protection against threats from perpetrators. The **Witness Protection Agency** offers protection until justice is served. You do not have to face this alone.
