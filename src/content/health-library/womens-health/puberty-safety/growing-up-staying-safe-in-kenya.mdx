## Introduction: Growing Up, Staying Safe in Kenya

Puberty is a time of change — your body grows, emotions shift and you begin to notice the world differently. For many young people in Kenya, this transition can be confusing, especially without accurate information. At the same time, threats to safety — in schools and communities — are real. Knowledge is your best protection.

**What Is Puberty?**

The time when your body matures from a child to an adult. Usually starts between ages **8–14 for girls** and **9–15 for boys**. These changes are driven by hormones — chemical messengers produced by glands in your body.

**Article 53 — Kenya Constitution**

Every child in Kenya has the right to be protected from abuse, neglect and all forms of violence under **Article 53 of the Constitution** and the **Children Act, 2022**. Puberty does not give anyone permission to touch you without your consent — your body belongs to you at every age.

💡 **Key Point**

Knowledge is your best protection. Understanding your body helps you recognise when something is wrong. Knowing your rights helps you speak up. Understanding consent helps you set boundaries.

## Body Changes for Girls

*Everyone develops at their own pace. Your friend may start her period at 10 and you may not start until 14 — both are normal. Comparing yourself to others only causes unnecessary worry.*

| Change | What Happens | When (Typical Age Range) |
|--------|-------------|--------------------------|
| **Breast development (Thelarche)** | Small buds form under the nipple, then breasts grow. Usually the *first visible sign* of puberty. | 8–13 years |
| **Pubic hair growth (Pubarche)** | Hair appears on the vulva, then underarms. Driven by adrenal androgens (adrenarche). | 8–14 years |
| **Growth spurt** | Rapid increase in height — slows significantly after menarche begins. | 9–14 years |
| **First period (Menarche)** | Menstruation begins — usually 2–3 years after breast budding. Cycles may be irregular for the first 1–2 years. | 10–16 years (average 12.5) |
| **Vaginal discharge** | Clear or whitish fluid appears — the vagina cleaning itself. Often 6–12 months before first period. | Typically before menarche |
| **Hips widen** | Body prepares for future childbearing — changes in fat distribution. | Throughout puberty |
| **Body odour** | Sweat glands become more active — especially in armpits. Daily bathing and deodorant help. | Throughout puberty |

⚠️ **Important — When to Seek Evaluation**

- Signs of puberty **before age 8** — should be evaluated for precocious puberty
- **No breast development by age 13** — requires evaluation for delayed puberty
- **No period by age 15** (primary amenorrhea) — requires medical evaluation
- It can take **up to 5 years** for cycles to become regular after menarche

## Body Changes for Boys

| Change | What Happens | When |
|--------|-------------|------|
| **Testes & scrotum grow** | Usually the first visible sign of puberty in boys | 9–14 years |
| **Penis grows** | Lengthens and thickens | 10–15 years |
| **Pubic hair grows** | Hair appears at base of penis, then spreads to armpits, face, legs | 9–15 years |
| **Voice deepens** | Voice may "crack and break" as the larynx grows — temporary and normal | 10–15 years |
| **Growth spurt** | Rapid increase in height; shoulders broaden; muscle mass increases | 10–16 years |
| **Wet dreams (nocturnal emissions)** | Ejaculation during sleep — involuntary and completely normal. Not a sign of anything wrong. | 11–16 years |

✨ **Good to Know**

Wet dreams are completely normal. They do not mean you are "bad" or that something is wrong. It is your body's way of releasing excess semen. This is a natural part of male puberty that happens to almost all boys.

## Vaginal Discharge: What's Normal?

*One of the first signs of puberty in girls is vaginal discharge — fluid that comes from the vagina. This can be alarming if you don't expect it. It is completely normal and a sign of a healthy vagina doing its job.*

### Normal Discharge

- Clear or white (like egg whites or milk)
- No strong smell (or very mild)
- May stain underwear — slightly acidic, bleaches fabric (use panty liners if it bothers you)
- Changes throughout the menstrual cycle: stretchy and clear around ovulation; thicker and whiter before period

---

### See a Provider If

🚨 Seek care if discharge is:

- **Yellow, green, or grey**
- Has a **strong, foul or fishy smell**
- Accompanied by **itching, burning or swelling** around the vulva
- Causing **pain when urinating**
- Frothy or looks like cottage cheese

A simple swab test gives the diagnosis.

💡 A little discharge is normal — it is the vagina cleaning itself. But if it changes colour, smell or causes discomfort, see a healthcare provider. Poor toilet hygiene is a common cause of irritation in young girls. **Always wipe from front to back** to prevent bacteria from the anus reaching the vagina.

## Menstruation: What to Expect

**What Happens During a Period**

- The **uterine lining sheds** because no pregnancy occurred
- Bleeding typically lasts **3–7 days**
- Flow is heaviest in the **first 1–2 days**
- Cycles may be **irregular for the first 1–2 years** — normal
- Average blood loss: **30–80 mL** (about 2–6 tablespoons)

**Common Period Symptoms**

- Cramping in lower belly
- Backache
- Bloating
- Mood changes
- Fatigue

*Mild discomfort is common. Pain that stops you going to school or living your life is NOT normal and deserves medical attention.*

🇰🇪 **Kenya Context — Menstrual Dignity**

A study in Kibera found that while **80% of school-going adolescent girls know menstruation is normal**, many still lack access to clean water and private sanitation facilities at school. This makes managing periods with dignity difficult. If your school lacks facilities, speak to a teacher or parent — **you have a right to learn in dignity**.

## Hygiene During Puberty

### For Girls

- Change sanitary pads every **4–6 hours** (more often if flow is heavy)
- Wash the vulva with **water only** — no soap inside
- Wipe **front to back** after using the toilet
- Wear **breathable cotton underwear**
- Dispose of used pads properly — **wrap and bin, don't flush**

---

### For Boys

- Wash the penis gently with water and mild soap
- If uncircumcised, gently pull back foreskin and clean underneath
- Change underwear daily

**For Everyone:**

- Shower or bathe daily, especially after sports
- Use deodorant if body odour bothers you
- Wash hands after using the toilet

## Body Autonomy: I Own My Body

*Body autonomy means your body belongs to you. No one else has the right to touch it, use it, or make decisions about it without your permission — at any age, in any situation.*

### 4 Key Principles of Body Autonomy

**1. You are the boss of your body.** No one — not a relative, teacher, friend or stranger — has the right to touch you in ways that make you uncomfortable.

**2. Consent must be freely given.** Saying "yes" because you're scared, pressured or tricked is NOT real consent.

**3. Consent can be withdrawn anytime.** Even if you said yes before, you can change your mind.

**4. You have the right to say "NO."** A simple "No" is enough — you don't need to explain.

---

### Safe Touch vs Unsafe Touch

**Safe Touch:** Feels good, caring, helpful. Characteristics: respectful, not secret, not threatening.

Examples: A parent helping you bathe when young; a doctor examining you with a parent present; a welcome hug; a pat on the back.

**Unsafe Touch:** Feels scary, confusing or painful. Characteristics: secret, forced, makes you feel dirty or scared.

Examples: Anyone touching your private parts; being asked to touch someone else's private parts; kisses that make you uncomfortable; being shown sexual pictures.

✨ **Good to Know — Body Part Names Protect Children**

Teaching children the proper names for body parts (penis, vagina, breasts, bottom) is protective — not shameful. Abusers often target children who do not have words for their body parts, making it harder for the child to report what happened. Naming is a safety tool.

## The No-Go-Tell Rule

### Step 1 — NO

Say "No" loudly and clearly. "Stop that!" "I don't like that!" "Don't touch me!" You do not need to be polite — say NO firmly.

### Step 2 — GO

Get away from the situation as quickly as possible. Run to a safe place. Leave immediately — do not wait to see what happens next.

### Step 3 — TELL

Tell a trusted adult until someone helps you. **Keep telling** until someone believes you and takes action. Do not give up.

💡 **Safe Secrets vs Unsafe Secrets**

Safe secrets can be kept (like a surprise birthday party). **Unsafe secrets** that make you feel bad, scared or confused should **never** be kept — tell a trusted adult immediately. An adult who asks a child to keep a secret about touching is always wrong, no matter who they are.
