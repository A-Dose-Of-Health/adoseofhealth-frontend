## Period Problems: Bleeding Issues

*Period problems are any changes or symptoms related to your menstrual cycle that cause discomfort, disrupt your life or signal an underlying health condition. Up to 1 in 5 people with uteruses will experience a menstrual disorder at some point. Dismissing severe period pain as "just part of being a woman" is the single biggest reason conditions like endometriosis go undiagnosed for years. Your pain is valid.*

---

### 1. Heavy Menstrual Bleeding (Menorrhagia)

**Signs of Heavy Bleeding**

- Soaking through a pad or tampon **every hour for several consecutive hours**
- Needing to "double-up" on protection
- Passing large blood clots — bigger than a **10-shilling coin**
- Bleeding lasting **more than 7 days**
- Feeling tired, short of breath or lightheaded (signs of iron-deficiency anaemia)

*Normal average: 2–5 moderately soaked pads per day. Using more than 5–6 fully soaked pads is considered heavy.*

**Causes & Treatment**

**Causes:** Hormonal imbalances; uterine fibroids; uterine polyps; adenomyosis; bleeding disorders (von Willebrand disease); certain medications (blood thinners).

**Medications:**
- *Tranexamic acid* — reduces blood loss by helping clots form
- *NSAIDs (ibuprofen)* — reduce pain and can slightly decrease flow
- *Hormonal options* — pills, hormonal IUD, progestin tablets
- *Iron supplements* — often needed if anaemic

---

### 2. Irregular Periods (Oligomenorrhea)

Cycles that are consistently longer than 35 days or very unpredictable. Occasional irregularities are common during stress or illness. Consistent irregularity (month after month) needs investigation.

**Common Causes:** Stress; extreme exercise; eating disorders (low body weight); **PCOS**; thyroid disorders (under- or overactive); perimenopause (transition to menopause).

**Normal in the First Years:** In the first 1–2 years after your first period (menarche), cycles are often irregular as the body learns to ovulate. This is completely normal. By age 16, cycles should generally be predictable.

---

### 3. Absent Periods (Amenorrhea)

| Type | Definition | Common Causes |
|------|-----------|--------------| 
| **Primary Amenorrhea** | No period by age 15 | Hormonal disorders; structural abnormalities; genetic conditions |
| **Secondary Amenorrhea** | Missing periods for 3+ months after having regular cycles | Pregnancy (most common) · PCOS · thyroid disorder · high prolactin · low body weight · excessive exercise · premature ovarian insufficiency · pituitary tumours |

⚠️ **Important**

If you have missed your period for 3 months and are not pregnant, you need an evaluation. Prolonged absence of periods can lead to **bone density loss (osteoporosis)** over time because estrogen is essential for bone maintenance. This risk is real even in young women.

---

### 4. Bleeding Between Periods (Intermenstrual Bleeding)

Any bleeding that happens outside your regular period always has a cause — sometimes benign, but always requiring investigation.

| Type | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Mid-cycle spotting (around Day 14) | Can be normal ovulation spotting — track for 2+ cycles; see provider if heavy |
| After sex (post-coital bleeding) | Could be cervical issues (infection, polyp, inflammation) — requires evaluation |
| Caused by infections (cervicitis, STIs) | Requires STI testing and treatment |
| Cervical or uterine polyps/fibroids | Usually benign but needs confirmation by ultrasound or hysteroscopy |
| Cervical or uterine cancer | Less common but serious — any unexplained intermenstrual bleeding requires investigation |
| **After menopause — any amount** | **URGENT** — any post-menopausal bleeding requires immediate evaluation |

## Period Pain: Dysmenorrhea

### Primary Dysmenorrhea — "Normal" Cramping

**Cause:** Prostaglandins — chemicals that make the uterus contract to shed its lining. Highest on Day 1 of your period (which is why cramps are often worst then).

**Feels like:** Dull, throbbing cramping in the lower abdomen, radiating to lower back and thighs.

**When:** Starts just before or with your period, lasts 1–3 days.

**Response:** Usually responds well to OTC pain relief (ibuprofen better than paracetamol — targets prostaglandins).

---

### Secondary Dysmenorrhea — Pain Caused by a Condition

**Cause:** An underlying reproductive disorder — most commonly endometriosis, fibroids, adenomyosis or PID.

**Feels like:** Sharp, stabbing or persistent pain that may be present *even when not on your period*.

**When:** Often starts earlier in the cycle, lasts longer, and does not respond well to OTC painkillers.

**Action needed:** Requires medical investigation. Bring a pain diary to your appointment — note timing, severity (1–10) and impact on your life.

## Kenya Myth-Busting: Period Problems

| ❌ Myth | ✅ Truth |
|--------|---------|
| "If you have period pain, just bear it — it means you are a 'real woman'" | Pain that stops you from functioning is a medical symptom, not a rite of passage. Severe pain can be a sign of endometriosis that requires treatment. |
| "Heavy bleeding is just how you are — some women bleed more" | Bleeding heavy enough to cause anaemia or interfere with life is a medical problem, not a personality trait. |
| "Herbs can 'clean' the womb and fix irregular periods" | Irregular periods are caused by hormonal or structural issues. Herbs cannot fix a hormone imbalance or shrink a fibroid. Relying on herbs delays proper diagnosis. |
| "You cannot get pregnant if your periods are irregular" | Irregular periods mean you ovulate unpredictably, not that you never ovulate. You can still get pregnant — you still need contraception. |
| "Period blood is dirty blood that needs to be expelled" | Menstrual blood is the same blood in your body, mixed with uterine tissue. It is not "dirty" or "toxic." |
