# Menstrual Health

## The Menstrual Cycle — Your Body's Vital Sign

Menstruation (a "period") is the monthly shedding of the uterine lining when no pregnancy occurs. But a period is more than just bleeding — it is the visible end of a complex hormonal cycle that affects your energy, mood and health every single day. Leading medical organisations including the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists now recognise the menstrual cycle as a **vital sign**, just like pulse or blood pressure.

**Normal Cycle Facts**

- Average cycle: **28 days** (21–35 days is normal for adults)
- Bleeding lasts: **3–7 days**
- Blood loss: **30–80 mL** (2–6 tablespoons)
- Flow heaviest: first 1–2 days
- Key is **regularity for you** — not matching a textbook average

**The Hormonal Symphony**

Hypothalamus → releases GnRH → Pituitary → releases FSH & LH → Ovaries → produce estrogen & progesterone → Feedback loop, also influenced by thyroid and adrenal glands. Stress, illness and sleep can disrupt this entire chain.

**Cycle as Vital Sign**

An irregular or painful cycle can signal underlying conditions including: hormonal imbalances (thyroid, PCOS); bleeding disorders; structural issues (fibroids, polyps); infections or inflammatory conditions (endometriosis). Tracking it is not just about knowing your next period date — it is about understanding what your body is telling you.

💡 **Start Tracking Today**

Track your cycle on a calendar, in a notebook or with an app. Note the dates, flow level, type of pain and how it affects your life. This is your evidence — bring it to a provider. You are the expert on your own body.

## The Four Phases of Your Cycle

### Phase 1 — Menstruation (Days 1–5)

Estrogen and progesterone drop sharply. The uterine lining has no hormones to sustain it, so it breaks down and sheds — this is your period. Triggered by the collapse of the corpus luteum.

*Cramping · Fatigue · Low back pain*

---

### Phase 2 — Follicular Phase (Days 1–13, overlaps with menstruation)

FSH stimulates several follicles (tiny sacs containing eggs) to mature. One becomes dominant and produces increasing amounts of estrogen, thickening the uterine lining. Energy slowly returns.

*Energy returns · Mood lifts · Skin clears*

---

### Phase 3 — Ovulation (Around Day 14 in a 28-day cycle)

Estrogen peaks → LH surge → dominant follicle releases a mature egg. The egg lives 12–24 hours after release. Sperm can survive up to 5 days — so the fertile window is ~6 days total.

*Mittelschmerz · Clear stretchy mucus · Higher libido*

---

### Phase 4 — Luteal Phase (Days 15–28)

The empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum, producing large amounts of progesterone to prepare the uterine lining for possible pregnancy. If no pregnancy, the corpus luteum degenerates, progesterone falls and the cycle begins again.

*PMS symptoms · Bloating · Breast tenderness · Mood shifts*

---

## Signs of Ovulation — How to Recognise It

| Sign | What It Looks Like | How to Track |
|------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Cervical mucus** | Clear, slippery, stretchy like raw egg white — "EWCM" (egg white cervical mucus) is the most fertile sign | Check daily with toilet paper or clean fingers |
| **Basal body temperature (BBT)** | Temperature rises slightly (0.3–0.6°C) *after* ovulation due to progesterone — confirms ovulation already occurred, does not predict it | Take temperature every morning before getting out of bed with a BBT thermometer |
| **Ovulation pain (Mittelschmerz)** | Mild twinge or cramp on one side of lower abdomen — lasts minutes to hours | Note in diary or app — the side alternates each month |
| **Ovulation predictor kits (OPKs)** | Detect the LH surge that happens 24–36 hours before ovulation | Available at pharmacies; urine test similar to a pregnancy test |

## Menstrual Hygiene & Products

| Product | How to Use It | Important Notes |
|---------|--------------|----------------|
| **Pads** | Apply to underwear; change every 4–6 hours or more if flow is heavy | Wrap used disposables and bin them — never flush. Avoid scented varieties. |
| **Tampons** | Insert into vagina; change every 4–8 hours; use the *lowest absorbency* that works for your flow | **Never wear a single tampon for more than 8 hours** — reduces risk of Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS) |
| **Menstrual Cups/Discs** | Insert and seal; rinse after each use; at end of period, sanitise by boiling in water for 1–2 minutes | Reusable and cost-effective. Emptying twice daily is usually sufficient. Takes practice to master. |
| **Period Underwear** | Change every 6–12 hours (more if flow is heavy); rinse immediately after removing; machine wash per instructions | Higher upfront cost but washable and reusable. Good for backup or light days. |

## Self-Care for Cramps & Pain

### Heat Therapy

Use a hot water bottle, heating pad or warm bath with Epsom salts on your lower abdomen. Be careful not to burn yourself — prolonged exposure to a heat source can cause **Erythema Ab Igne (EAI)**, a skin rash from repeated heat exposure.

---

### OTC Medication

**Ibuprofen** is often better for cramps than paracetamol because it reduces **prostaglandins** (the chemicals that cause the uterus to contract). **Buscopan** can also help. Always take with food and at the recommended dose — if your prescribed dose isn't working, tell your provider rather than taking more.

---

### Movement & Massage

Light yoga, stretching or walking can ease pain and boost mood by releasing endorphins. Gentle circular massage on your lower back can relax tight muscles. Exercise may feel like the last thing you want to do — but gentle movement genuinely helps.

## Period Problems: Bleeding Issues

*Period problems are any changes or symptoms related to your menstrual cycle that cause discomfort, disrupt your life or signal an underlying health condition. Up to 1 in 5 people with uteruses will experience a menstrual disorder at some point. Dismissing severe period pain as "just part of being a woman" is the single biggest reason conditions like endometriosis go undiagnosed for years. Your pain is valid.*

---

### 1. Heavy Menstrual Bleeding (Menorrhagia)

**Signs of Heavy Bleeding**

- Soaking through a pad or tampon **every hour for several consecutive hours**
- Needing to "double-up" on protection
- Passing large blood clots — bigger than a **10-shilling coin**
- Bleeding lasting **more than 7 days**
- Feeling tired, short of breath or lightheaded (signs of iron-deficiency anaemia)

*Normal average: 2–5 moderately soaked pads per day. Using more than 5–6 fully soaked pads is considered heavy.*

**Causes & Treatment**

**Causes:** Hormonal imbalances; uterine fibroids; uterine polyps; adenomyosis; bleeding disorders (von Willebrand disease); certain medications (blood thinners).

**Medications:**
- *Tranexamic acid* — reduces blood loss by helping clots form
- *NSAIDs (ibuprofen)* — reduce pain and can slightly decrease flow
- *Hormonal options* — pills, hormonal IUD, progestin tablets
- *Iron supplements* — often needed if anaemic

---

### 2. Irregular Periods (Oligomenorrhea)

Cycles that are consistently longer than 35 days or very unpredictable. Occasional irregularities are common during stress or illness. Consistent irregularity (month after month) needs investigation.

**Common Causes:** Stress; extreme exercise; eating disorders (low body weight); **PCOS**; thyroid disorders (under- or overactive); perimenopause (transition to menopause).

**Normal in the First Years:** In the first 1–2 years after your first period (menarche), cycles are often irregular as the body learns to ovulate. This is completely normal. By age 16, cycles should generally be predictable.

---

### 3. Absent Periods (Amenorrhea)

| Type | Definition | Common Causes |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| **Primary Amenorrhea** | No period by age 15 | Hormonal disorders; structural abnormalities; genetic conditions |
| **Secondary Amenorrhea** | Missing periods for 3+ months after having regular cycles | Pregnancy (most common) · PCOS · thyroid disorder · high prolactin · low body weight · excessive exercise · premature ovarian insufficiency · pituitary tumours |

⚠️ **Important**

If you have missed your period for 3 months and are not pregnant, you need an evaluation. Prolonged absence of periods can lead to **bone density loss (osteoporosis)** over time because estrogen is essential for bone maintenance. This risk is real even in young women.

---

### 4. Bleeding Between Periods (Intermenstrual Bleeding)

Any bleeding that happens outside your regular period always has a cause — sometimes benign, but always requiring investigation.

| Type | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Mid-cycle spotting (around Day 14) | Can be normal ovulation spotting — track for 2+ cycles; see provider if heavy |
| After sex (post-coital bleeding) | Could be cervical issues (infection, polyp, inflammation) — requires evaluation |
| Caused by infections (cervicitis, STIs) | Requires STI testing and treatment |
| Cervical or uterine polyps/fibroids | Usually benign but needs confirmation by ultrasound or hysteroscopy |
| Cervical or uterine cancer | Less common but serious — any unexplained intermenstrual bleeding requires investigation |
| **After menopause — any amount** | **URGENT** — any post-menopausal bleeding requires immediate evaluation |

## Period Pain: Dysmenorrhea

### Primary Dysmenorrhea — "Normal" Cramping

**Cause:** Prostaglandins — chemicals that make the uterus contract to shed its lining. Highest on Day 1 of your period (which is why cramps are often worst then).

**Feels like:** Dull, throbbing cramping in the lower abdomen, radiating to lower back and thighs.

**When:** Starts just before or with your period, lasts 1–3 days.

**Response:** Usually responds well to OTC pain relief (ibuprofen better than paracetamol — targets prostaglandins).

---

### Secondary Dysmenorrhea — Pain Caused by a Condition

**Cause:** An underlying reproductive disorder — most commonly endometriosis, fibroids, adenomyosis or PID.

**Feels like:** Sharp, stabbing or persistent pain that may be present *even when not on your period*.

**When:** Often starts earlier in the cycle, lasts longer, and does not respond well to OTC painkillers.

**Action needed:** Requires medical investigation. Bring a pain diary to your appointment — note timing, severity (1–10) and impact on your life.

## Kenya Myth-Busting: Period Problems

| ❌ Myth | ✅ Truth |
|--------|---------|
| "If you have period pain, just bear it — it means you are a 'real woman'" | Pain that stops you from functioning is a medical symptom, not a rite of passage. Severe pain can be a sign of endometriosis that requires treatment. |
| "Heavy bleeding is just how you are — some women bleed more" | Bleeding heavy enough to cause anaemia or interfere with life is a medical problem, not a personality trait. |
| "Herbs can 'clean' the womb and fix irregular periods" | Irregular periods are caused by hormonal or structural issues. Herbs cannot fix a hormone imbalance or shrink a fibroid. Relying on herbs delays proper diagnosis. |
| "You cannot get pregnant if your periods are irregular" | Irregular periods mean you ovulate unpredictably, not that you never ovulate. You can still get pregnant — you still need contraception. |
| "Period blood is dirty blood that needs to be expelled" | Menstrual blood is the same blood in your body, mixed with uterine tissue. It is not "dirty" or "toxic." |

## PMS & PMDD — The Pre-Menstrual Phase

*PMS is incredibly common — up to 75% of people who menstruate experience some form. PMDD is a severe, debilitating version affecting 3–8%. PMDD is recognised as a psychiatric condition — the brain has an abnormal reaction to normal hormonal changes. It is not a character flaw or weakness.*

| Feature | PMS | PMDD |
|---------|-----|------|
| Number of symptoms | One or more | At least 5 (including at least 1 mood symptom) |
| Mood impact | Mild to moderate | Severe — disrupts relationships, work and school |
| Anger/Irritability | Can occur, but manageable | Intense rage, anger, relationship conflict |
| Depression/Anxiety | Mild | Severe, hopeless, overwhelmed, panicky |
| Functioning | Able to function normally | Unable to function; needs to isolate |
| Duration | A few days before period | 1–2 weeks before period, relief after period starts |
| Key diagnostic clue | Predictable pattern each cycle | A "week of relief" after period starts — then symptoms return next luteal phase |

---

### PMDD Core Symptoms (DSM criteria — must include ≥1 of first 4)

**Must Include at Least One of These Four:**

1. Marked mood swings (sudden sadness, tearfulness)
2. Marked irritability or anger
3. Marked depressed mood, feelings of hopelessness
4. Marked anxiety, tension, feeling "on edge"

**Additional Possible Symptoms:**

5. Decreased interest in usual activities
6. Difficulty concentrating
7. Fatigue, lack of energy
8. Changes in appetite (overeating or specific cravings)
9. Hypersomnia (too much sleep) or insomnia
10. Feeling overwhelmed or out of control
11. Physical symptoms (breast tenderness, bloating, headaches)

---

## What Causes PMS & PMDD?

People with PMS/PMDD have *normal* levels of estrogen and progesterone. The problem is how their brain and body react to these normal fluctuations. Hormonal changes may trigger a drop in **serotonin** — the brain chemical regulating mood, sleep and pain. **Genetics** play a role: if your mother or sister had PMDD, you are more likely to have it.

---

## Management & Treatment

### Lifestyle (First-line for mild–moderate PMS)

**Nutrition:** Eat complex carbs (boost serotonin); increase **calcium to 1,200 mg/day** — studies show significant reduction in PMS symptoms (sources: milk, yoghurt, leafy greens, fortified plant milks); limit salt (reduces bloating); reduce caffeine and alcohol (worsen anxiety and breast tenderness).

**Exercise:** Regular aerobic activity releases endorphins, improves mood and reduces fatigue.

**Sleep:** Aim 7–9 hours; consistent bedtime helps regulate mood.

**Stress management:** Yoga, meditation, deep breathing, journaling.

---

### Medical Treatments (for PMDD or severe PMS)

**SSRIs (antidepressants)** — the **first-line treatment for PMDD**. Fluoxetine or sertraline can be taken continuously or only during the luteal phase (1–2 weeks before period). Work within *days* for PMDD (faster than for depression).

**Hormonal birth control** — pills suppressing ovulation (e.g., those containing drospirenone and ethinyl estradiol) can eliminate the hormonal cycle that triggers PMDD symptoms.

**GnRH agonists** — In severe cases, temporarily induce menopause to stop the hormonal cycle. Usually short-term.

⚠️ **When to Seek Urgent Help**

- If you have **thoughts of self-harm or suicide** related to your cycle — this is a medical emergency
- Mood symptoms are affecting your relationships, work or ability to function
- You feel hopeless, overwhelmed or out of control
- Lifestyle changes are not helping after 2–3 cycles

💡 **Diagnosing PMDD — The 4-Step Approach**

1. **Track symptoms daily** for at least two cycles — note mood, physical symptoms and severity.
2. **Look for the pattern** — do symptoms appear 1–2 weeks before your period and disappear after it starts?
3. **Book an appointment** with a gynaecologist, psychiatrist or primary care provider. Bring your symptom tracker.
4. **Be specific:** Say "I think I may have PMDD because my symptoms follow this pattern and they are severely affecting my life."
