## The Menstrual Cycle — Your Body's Vital Sign

Menstruation (a "period") is the monthly shedding of the uterine lining when no pregnancy occurs. But a period is more than just bleeding — it is the visible end of a complex hormonal cycle that affects your energy, mood and health every single day. Leading medical organisations including the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists now recognise the menstrual cycle as a **vital sign**, just like pulse or blood pressure.

**Normal Cycle Facts**

- Average cycle: **28 days** (21–35 days is normal for adults)
- Bleeding lasts: **3–7 days**
- Blood loss: **30–80 mL** (2–6 tablespoons)
- Flow heaviest: first 1–2 days
- Key is **regularity for you** — not matching a textbook average

**The Hormonal Symphony**

Hypothalamus → releases GnRH → Pituitary → releases FSH & LH → Ovaries → produce estrogen & progesterone → Feedback loop, also influenced by thyroid and adrenal glands. Stress, illness and sleep can disrupt this entire chain.

**Cycle as Vital Sign**

An irregular or painful cycle can signal underlying conditions including: hormonal imbalances (thyroid, PCOS); bleeding disorders; structural issues (fibroids, polyps); infections or inflammatory conditions (endometriosis). Tracking it is not just about knowing your next period date — it is about understanding what your body is telling you.

💡 **Start Tracking Today**

Track your cycle on a calendar, in a notebook or with an app. Note the dates, flow level, type of pain and how it affects your life. This is your evidence — bring it to a provider. You are the expert on your own body.

## The Four Phases of Your Cycle

### Phase 1 — Menstruation (Days 1–5)

Estrogen and progesterone drop sharply. The uterine lining has no hormones to sustain it, so it breaks down and sheds — this is your period. Triggered by the collapse of the corpus luteum.

*Cramping · Fatigue · Low back pain*

---

### Phase 2 — Follicular Phase (Days 1–13, overlaps with menstruation)

FSH stimulates several follicles (tiny sacs containing eggs) to mature. One becomes dominant and produces increasing amounts of estrogen, thickening the uterine lining. Energy slowly returns.

*Energy returns · Mood lifts · Skin clears*

---

### Phase 3 — Ovulation (Around Day 14 in a 28-day cycle)

Estrogen peaks → LH surge → dominant follicle releases a mature egg. The egg lives 12–24 hours after release. Sperm can survive up to 5 days — so the fertile window is ~6 days total.

*Mittelschmerz · Clear stretchy mucus · Higher libido*

---

### Phase 4 — Luteal Phase (Days 15–28)

The empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum, producing large amounts of progesterone to prepare the uterine lining for possible pregnancy. If no pregnancy, the corpus luteum degenerates, progesterone falls and the cycle begins again.

*PMS symptoms · Bloating · Breast tenderness · Mood shifts*

## Signs of Ovulation — How to Recognise It

| Sign | What It Looks Like | How to Track |
|------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Cervical mucus** | Clear, slippery, stretchy like raw egg white — "EWCM" (egg white cervical mucus) is the most fertile sign | Check daily with toilet paper or clean fingers |
| **Basal body temperature (BBT)** | Temperature rises slightly (0.3–0.6°C) *after* ovulation due to progesterone — confirms ovulation already occurred, does not predict it | Take temperature every morning before getting out of bed with a BBT thermometer |
| **Ovulation pain (Mittelschmerz)** | Mild twinge or cramp on one side of lower abdomen — lasts minutes to hours | Note in diary or app — the side alternates each month |
| **Ovulation predictor kits (OPKs)** | Detect the LH surge that happens 24–36 hours before ovulation | Available at pharmacies; urine test similar to a pregnancy test |

## Menstrual Hygiene & Products

| Product | How to Use It | Important Notes |
|---------|--------------|----------------|
| **Pads** | Apply to underwear; change every 4–6 hours or more if flow is heavy | Wrap used disposables and bin them — never flush. Avoid scented varieties. |
| **Tampons** | Insert into vagina; change every 4–8 hours; use the *lowest absorbency* that works for your flow | **Never wear a single tampon for more than 8 hours** — reduces risk of Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS) |
| **Menstrual Cups/Discs** | Insert and seal; rinse after each use; at end of period, sanitise by boiling in water for 1–2 minutes | Reusable and cost-effective. Emptying twice daily is usually sufficient. Takes practice to master. |
| **Period Underwear** | Change every 6–12 hours (more if flow is heavy); rinse immediately after removing; machine wash per instructions | Higher upfront cost but washable and reusable. Good for backup or light days. |

## Self-Care for Cramps & Pain

### Heat Therapy

Use a hot water bottle, heating pad or warm bath with Epsom salts on your lower abdomen. Be careful not to burn yourself — prolonged exposure to a heat source can cause **Erythema Ab Igne (EAI)**, a skin rash from repeated heat exposure.

---

### OTC Medication

**Ibuprofen** is often better for cramps than paracetamol because it reduces **prostaglandins** (the chemicals that cause the uterus to contract). **Buscopan** can also help. Always take with food and at the recommended dose — if your prescribed dose isn't working, tell your provider rather than taking more.

---

### Movement & Massage

Light yoga, stretching or walking can ease pain and boost mood by releasing endorphins. Gentle circular massage on your lower back can relax tight muscles. Exercise may feel like the last thing you want to do — but gentle movement genuinely helps.
