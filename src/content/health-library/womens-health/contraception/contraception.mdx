# Contraception — Your Complete Guide

## Contraception: A Story About Choice

Contraception (family planning) allows you to prevent pregnancy and plan your family. Kenya has a wide range of methods available — from short-term to permanent. The "best" method is the one that works for your body, your lifestyle, your relationship and your future plans. There is no one-size-fits-all.

- **57%** — Married Kenyan women using a modern contraceptive method (KDHS 2022) — up from 32% two decades ago
- **14%** — Unmet need for contraception (women wanting to delay/stop childbearing but not using contraception) — down from 27% in 2003
- **88%** — Kenyan women now delivering in a health facility — a major improvement in maternal safety

💡 **The Golden Rule**

Only **condoms** protect against both pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV. If you are at risk of STIs, use condoms even if you are using another method for pregnancy prevention. This is called **dual method use**.

## Hormonal Methods — How They Work

*Hormonal methods use synthetic hormones to prevent pregnancy — primarily by stopping ovulation. They come in various forms to suit different lifestyles and needs. None protect against STIs.*

---

### 1. Combined Oral Contraceptive Pill (COC / "The Pill")

Contains estrogen + progestin. Works by:
- **Preventing ovulation** (primary mechanism)
- Thickening cervical mucus (blocks sperm)
- Thinning the uterine lining

**Perfect use:** >99% effective  
**Typical use:** ~91% (up to 7 in 100 users pregnant in first year)

A standard pack has 21 active pills, then a 7-day break with a withdrawal bleed. If started on Day 1 of period: immediate protection. Any other day: use condoms for 7 days.

**✅ Pros:** Makes periods regular, lighter, less painful; reduces risk of ovarian, uterine and colorectal cancers; improves acne; fertility returns immediately after stopping.

**❌ Cons:** Must be taken same time every day; doesn't protect against STIs; some medications (HIV, TB, epilepsy drugs) reduce effectiveness; not suitable for smokers over 35, high blood pressure, or blood clot history.

**🚨 See a doctor if:** Severe leg pain/swelling (possible clot); chest pain or shortness of breath; severe headaches or vision changes; yellowing of skin/eyes; if you missed 2+ pills and had unprotected sex.

---

### 2. Progestogen-Only Pill (POP / Mini-Pill)

Contains only progestogen — works primarily by thickening cervical mucus. Some types (containing desogestrel) also stop ovulation.

**Critical difference:** Must be taken at the *same time every day* — the "missed pill" window is only **3–12 hours** depending on the type (vs 24 hours for the COC). Set a daily alarm.

**Best for:** Women who cannot take estrogen; breastfeeding mothers (safe immediately after birth); women with migraines with aura or high blood pressure.

**✅ Pros:** Safe while breastfeeding; can start immediately after childbirth, abortion or miscarriage; fertility returns immediately after stopping; suitable when estrogen is contraindicated.

**❌ Cons:** Irregular bleeding very common; requires strict timing; some medications interfere with effectiveness.

**If late:** If more than 3 hours late (or 12 hours for desogestrel pills) — follow missed pill instructions on package and use condoms for 2 days.

---

### 3. Contraceptive Injection (Depo-Provera / Sayana Press)

A progestin injection into the muscle (buttock or arm) — given every **3 months** by a healthcare provider. Works primarily by stopping ovulation.

**Effectiveness:** 94–99%  
**Kenya cost:** ~KSh 100 per injection at public facilities

One of the most popular methods in Kenya — private, convenient, only needed 4 times a year.

**✅ Pros:** Highly effective; private; periods often become lighter or stop (benefit for many); safe while breastfeeding.

**❌ Cons:** Requires clinic visit every 3 months; delay in return of fertility after stopping (may take several months to over a year); possible weight gain; irregular bleeding early on.

**Mark your calendar:** If more than 2 weeks late for your next injection, you may need pregnancy testing and backup contraception. Bone density may decrease with very long-term use — reversible after stopping.

---

### 4. Contraceptive Implant (Implanon / Jadelle)

A small, thin, flexible rod (matchstick-sized) inserted under the skin of the upper arm by a trained provider. Releases progestin steadily. Lasts **3–5 years** depending on type.

**Effectiveness: >99%** — one of the most reliable methods available

**"Fit and forget"** — once inserted, you don't have to think about it for years, yet it is fully reversible. Fertility returns quickly after removal. Not visible to others.

**✅ Pros:** >99% effective; long-acting; reversible; discreet; safe for those who cannot use estrogen; LARC (Long-Acting Reversible Contraception).

**❌ Cons:** Irregular bleeding is very common (most common side effect — periods may become lighter, heavier, longer or stop); some HIV/TB/epilepsy medications reduce effectiveness; requires trained provider.

**Kenya cost:** ~KSh 300 for insertion/removal at public facilities like Kiambu Level 5.

---

### 5. Contraceptive Patch & Vaginal Ring

**The Patch**

A small beige sticky patch releasing estrogen + progestin through the skin. Applied weekly for 3 weeks, then 1 patch-free week. **~91% effective with typical use**.

✅ Only need to remember it weekly. ❌ Less effective in women over 90 kg; may be visible; less widely available in Kenya.

Shares the same benefits and risks as the combined pill (including small blood clot risk).

**The Vaginal Ring (NuvaRing)**

A small flexible plastic ring inserted into the vagina for 3 weeks, then removed for 1 week. Releases estrogen + progestin. **~91% typical use, >97% perfect use**.

✅ Monthly convenience; user-controlled; lower systemic side effects for some women. ❌ Requires comfort with self-insertion; not widely available in Kenya.

If ring falls out: rinse with cool water and reinsert within 3 hours to maintain protection.

## Long-Acting Reversible Contraception (LARCs)

*LARCs are the most effective contraceptive methods available — and they are fully reversible. They are recommended for most women, including adolescents and women who have not had children. Return to fertility is prompt after removal.*

---

### Copper IUD — Non-Hormonal LARC

A small T-shaped plastic device wrapped in copper wire, inserted into the uterus by a trained provider. Copper is toxic to sperm — impairs their ability to move and fertilise an egg. Also causes a slight change in the uterine lining.

**Effectiveness: >99%** — one of the most reliable methods  
**Duration:** 5–12 years (depending on brand)  
**Kenya cost:** ~KSh 200 for insertion/removal  
**Also the most effective emergency contraception** — if inserted within 5 days of unprotected sex.

**✅ Pros:** >99% effective; hormone-free (great for those who can't or prefer not to use hormones); fertility returns immediately after removal; does not affect natural cycle, mood or weight.

**❌ Cons:** May cause heavier, longer, more painful periods (especially first 3–6 months — usually improves); small risk of expulsion or perforation (rare).

**🚨 See a doctor if:** Can't feel IUD strings; feel hard plastic coming out; severe pelvic pain, fever or unusual discharge (possible infection); positive pregnancy test with IUD in place.

---

### Hormonal IUS (Mirena) — Hormonal LARC

A small T-shaped device releasing **levonorgestrel (progestin) locally** into the uterus. Works by thickening cervical mucus, thinning the uterine lining, and sometimes preventing ovulation.

**Effectiveness: >99%** · **Duration: 3–8 years**

Because the hormone is released directly into the uterus, very little enters the bloodstream — **significantly fewer systemic side effects** than pills or injections.

**Key Advantage — Heavy Periods**

The **IUS is a game-changer for heavy, painful periods**. It dramatically reduces menstrual bleeding — many women have very light periods or no periods at all after the first year. It is also used therapeutically for endometriosis, adenomyosis and fibroids (as part of symptom management).

**❌ Cons:** Irregular bleeding/spotting common in first 3–6 months; requires trained provider; more expensive than copper IUD; less widely available in Kenya.

## Barrier Methods

*Only condoms protect against STIs.*

---

### Male Condom

**How to Use Correctly**

- Put it on **before any genital contact** (pre-ejaculatory fluid can contain sperm)
- Pinch the tip to leave space for semen
- Roll down to the base of the erect penis
- After ejaculation, hold the rim and withdraw while still erect
- **New condom for every act of sex**
- Always check the expiry date
- Use **water-based or silicone-based lubricant only** — oil-based products weaken latex and cause breakage
- Available free at public health facilities, inexpensively at pharmacies

**Effectiveness:** 98% perfect use / 85% typical use

**The only method that protects against both pregnancy AND STIs/HIV.**

✅ No hormones; widely available; affordable; different sizes and textures available; can be used *alongside* other methods for dual protection.

❌ Must be used correctly every time; can interrupt spontaneity; some people have latex allergy (non-latex options available — polyurethane or polyisoprene).

*Latex allergy? Non-latex condoms are available — ask your provider or pharmacist.*

---

### Female Condom

A soft polyurethane pouch with flexible rings at each end, worn inside the vagina. **Effectiveness: 79–95%**. It protects against both pregnancy and STIs/HIV — and it is **woman-controlled**, giving women the power to protect themselves without relying on a partner. Can be inserted up to 8 hours before sex. Made of polyurethane — safe for latex allergies and not affected by oil-based lubricants. Requires practice to insert correctly.

---

### Diaphragm & Cervical Cap

A reusable dome-shaped silicone cup inserted into the vagina to cover the cervix before sex — must be used with spermicide. **~88% effective with perfect use**. Must be fitted by a trained provider. Hormone-free and reusable (lasts 1–2 years). May increase UTI risk. Not widely available in Kenya — larger private clinics are most likely to offer fitting.

## Natural & Permanent Methods

---

### Fertility Awareness Methods (FAMs)

Track biological signs to identify the fertile window. To avoid pregnancy: abstain or use a barrier method on fertile days. To achieve pregnancy: time sex to coincide with the fertile window.

**Signs tracked:**
- *Cervical mucus* — clear, slippery, egg-white on fertile days
- *BBT* — temperature rise after ovulation (confirms it occurred)
- *Calendar tracking* — for women with very regular cycles
- *OPKs* — for predicting the LH surge

**Effectiveness:** 99% with perfect use; 76–88% typical use. Requires daily dedication and discipline.

**✅ Pros:** No hormones, no medical procedures, no cost; acceptable to all cultures and religions; increases body awareness; can be used to either avoid or achieve pregnancy.

**❌ Cons:** NOT a "lazy" method — requires daily tracking and strict discipline; difficult with irregular cycles; requires abstinence or backup method on fertile days (~half the cycle); does not protect against STIs; less effective than LARCs or hormonal methods with typical use.

**Best for:** Highly motivated, organised couples with regular cycles. Learn from a trained instructor before relying on it. Use condoms as backup during the learning phase.

---

### Lactational Amenorrhea Method (LAM)

Exclusive breastfeeding suppresses ovulation and can prevent pregnancy — **98% effective when ALL THREE conditions are met:**

1. You are **exclusively breastfeeding** (no supplements, no solid foods, no formula)
2. Your baby is **under 6 months old**
3. Your **periods have not returned**

If any one condition changes, switch to another contraceptive method immediately. Free and supports breastfeeding — but only works with strict adherence to all three criteria.

---

### Permanent Methods — Sterilisation

| Method | What It Involves | Key Facts |
|--------|----------------|----------|
| **Female Sterilisation (Tubal Ligation)** | Surgical procedure (usually laparoscopic/"keyhole") permanently blocking or sealing the fallopian tubes. Can be done immediately after childbirth. Done under anaesthesia. | >99% effective. Permanent. Does NOT affect hormones, sex drive or sexual pleasure. Normal periods continue. Low cost at public hospitals (subsidised). Reversal is difficult, expensive and not always successful — only choose this if 100% certain about no more children. |
| **Male Sterilisation (Vasectomy)** | Simple 15–20 minute procedure under local anaesthetic blocking the vas deferens (tubes carrying sperm). Simpler, safer and cheaper than female sterilisation. | >99% effective. Does NOT affect testosterone, sex drive, erections or ejaculation — semen is produced normally, just without sperm. Takes 3 months/20 ejaculations to clear remaining sperm. Semen test needed to confirm success before stopping other contraception. Underutilised in Kenya due to cultural myths — but safe and highly effective. A powerful act of shared responsibility. |

## Emergency Contraception (EC)

*Emergency contraception prevents pregnancy after unprotected sex or contraceptive failure (broken condom, missed pills). It is NOT the abortion pill — it prevents pregnancy from occurring by delaying or inhibiting ovulation. It will NOT work if you are already pregnant. It does NOT protect against STIs.*

| Method | How Long After Sex | Effectiveness & Key Facts |
|--------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **Levonorgestrel pills (Postinor-2)** *Available OTC in Kenya · KSh 200–400* | Within **72 hours** (3 days) — the sooner, the better | Up to 95% effective if taken within 24 hours; ~85% at 48–72 hours. Effectiveness decreases with time. May cause nausea, headache, irregular bleeding. If you vomit within 2 hours, take another dose. |
| **Ulipristal acetate (ellaOne)** *Requires prescription* | Within **120 hours** (5 days) | Remains consistently effective throughout the 5-day window — better than levonorgestrel, especially closer to ovulation. More effective in women with higher body weight. |
| **Copper IUD** *Requires clinic visit* | Within **5 days** | **Most effective EC (>99%)**. Also provides ongoing contraception for 5–12 years. No hormones. May cause heavier periods. Best option if you were considering an IUD anyway. |

💡 **Awareness in Kenya**

A study in Nairobi found that although EC (Postinor-2) is registered in Kenya, it was scarcely known or used. Awareness has grown since, but many women and girls still do not know about EC or where to access it. **It is available over-the-counter at most pharmacies without a prescription (levonorgestrel).** The sooner you act, the more effective it will be — do not wait.

⚠️ **EC and HIV Risk**

Emergency contraception does NOT protect against HIV or other STIs. If you had unprotected sex with a partner whose HIV status you don't know, you may also need **PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis)** — which must be started within **72 hours** of exposure. Go to a hospital immediately and ask about PEP.

## Myth-Busting: Contraception in Kenya

| ❌ Myth | ✅ Truth |
|--------|---------|
| "Contraceptives cause infertility" | Modern contraceptives do not cause infertility. For most methods, fertility returns immediately or within a few months after stopping. The only exception is sterilisation, which is intended to be permanent. |
| "If you're breastfeeding, you can't get pregnant" | Only true if ALL three LAM conditions are met: exclusively breastfeeding, baby under 6 months, AND periods have not returned. If any condition is missing, you need additional contraception. |
| "Contraceptives cause cancer" | The combined pill slightly increases breast and cervical cancer risk, but also significantly reduces ovarian, endometrial and colorectal cancer risk. For most women, benefits outweigh risks. Discuss your personal risk with a provider. |
| "Women who use contraceptives are promiscuous" | Women use contraception for many valid reasons: to space children, complete education, protect health, plan families. Contraception is a tool for empowerment, not a moral judgement. |
| "Emergency contraception is the abortion pill" | EC prevents pregnancy by delaying ovulation — it does not work if a woman is already pregnant. The abortion pill (mifepristone) ends an existing pregnancy. They are completely different medications. |
| "Men won't agree to family planning" | Many men in Kenya support family planning when they understand its benefits. Engaging men as partners — through education, counselling and couples' visits — improves outcomes for everyone. |
| "Long-acting methods are not suitable for young women or those who haven't had children" | LARCs like implants and IUDs are safe and recommended for most women, including adolescents and nulliparous women. They are among the most effective methods available and fully reversible. |
