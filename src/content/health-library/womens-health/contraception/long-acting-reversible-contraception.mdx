## Long-Acting Reversible Contraception (LARCs)

*LARCs are the most effective contraceptive methods available — and they are fully reversible. They are recommended for most women, including adolescents and women who have not had children. Return to fertility is prompt after removal.*

---

### Copper IUD — Non-Hormonal LARC

A small T-shaped plastic device wrapped in copper wire, inserted into the uterus by a trained provider. Copper is toxic to sperm — impairs their ability to move and fertilise an egg. Also causes a slight change in the uterine lining.

**Effectiveness: >99%** — one of the most reliable methods  
**Duration:** 5–12 years (depending on brand)  
**Kenya cost:** ~KSh 200 for insertion/removal  
**Also the most effective emergency contraception** — if inserted within 5 days of unprotected sex.

**✅ Pros:** >99% effective; hormone-free (great for those who can't or prefer not to use hormones); fertility returns immediately after removal; does not affect natural cycle, mood or weight.

**❌ Cons:** May cause heavier, longer, more painful periods (especially first 3–6 months — usually improves); small risk of expulsion or perforation (rare).

**🚨 See a doctor if:** Can't feel IUD strings; feel hard plastic coming out; severe pelvic pain, fever or unusual discharge (possible infection); positive pregnancy test with IUD in place.

---

### Hormonal IUS (Mirena) — Hormonal LARC

A small T-shaped device releasing **levonorgestrel (progestin) locally** into the uterus. Works by thickening cervical mucus, thinning the uterine lining, and sometimes preventing ovulation.

**Effectiveness: >99%** · **Duration: 3–8 years**

Because the hormone is released directly into the uterus, very little enters the bloodstream — **significantly fewer systemic side effects** than pills or injections.

**Key Advantage — Heavy Periods**

The **IUS is a game-changer for heavy, painful periods**. It dramatically reduces menstrual bleeding — many women have very light periods or no periods at all after the first year. It is also used therapeutically for endometriosis, adenomyosis and fibroids (as part of symptom management).

**❌ Cons:** Irregular bleeding/spotting common in first 3–6 months; requires trained provider; more expensive than copper IUD; less widely available in Kenya.
