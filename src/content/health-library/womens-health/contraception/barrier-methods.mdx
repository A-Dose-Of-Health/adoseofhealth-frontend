## Barrier Methods

*Only condoms protect against STIs.*

---

### Male Condom

**How to Use Correctly**

- Put it on **before any genital contact** (pre-ejaculatory fluid can contain sperm)
- Pinch the tip to leave space for semen
- Roll down to the base of the erect penis
- After ejaculation, hold the rim and withdraw while still erect
- **New condom for every act of sex**
- Always check the expiry date
- Use **water-based or silicone-based lubricant only** — oil-based products weaken latex and cause breakage
- Available free at public health facilities, inexpensively at pharmacies

**Effectiveness:** 98% perfect use / 85% typical use

**The only method that protects against both pregnancy AND STIs/HIV.**

✅ No hormones; widely available; affordable; different sizes and textures available; can be used *alongside* other methods for dual protection.

❌ Must be used correctly every time; can interrupt spontaneity; some people have latex allergy (non-latex options available — polyurethane or polyisoprene).

*Latex allergy? Non-latex condoms are available — ask your provider or pharmacist.*

---

### Female Condom

A soft polyurethane pouch with flexible rings at each end, worn inside the vagina. **Effectiveness: 79–95%**. It protects against both pregnancy and STIs/HIV — and it is **woman-controlled**, giving women the power to protect themselves without relying on a partner. Can be inserted up to 8 hours before sex. Made of polyurethane — safe for latex allergies and not affected by oil-based lubricants. Requires practice to insert correctly.

---

### Diaphragm & Cervical Cap

A reusable dome-shaped silicone cup inserted into the vagina to cover the cervix before sex — must be used with spermicide. **~88% effective with perfect use**. Must be fitted by a trained provider. Hormone-free and reusable (lasts 1–2 years). May increase UTI risk. Not widely available in Kenya — larger private clinics are most likely to offer fitting.
