## Emergency Contraception (EC)

*Emergency contraception prevents pregnancy after unprotected sex or contraceptive failure (broken condom, missed pills). It is NOT the abortion pill — it prevents pregnancy from occurring by delaying or inhibiting ovulation. It will NOT work if you are already pregnant. It does NOT protect against STIs.*

---

### EC Methods Compared

| Method | How Long After Sex | Effectiveness & Key Facts |
|--------|------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Levonorgestrel pills (Postinor-2)** — Available OTC in Kenya · KSh 200–400 | Within **72 hours** (3 days) — the sooner, the better | Up to 95% effective if taken within 24 hours; ~85% at 48–72 hours. Effectiveness decreases with time. May cause nausea, headache, irregular bleeding. If you vomit within 2 hours, take another dose. |
| **Ulipristal acetate (ellaOne)** — Requires prescription | Within **120 hours** (5 days) | Remains consistently effective throughout the 5-day window — better than levonorgestrel, especially closer to ovulation. More effective in women with higher body weight. |
| **Copper IUD** — Requires clinic visit | Within **5 days** | **Most effective EC (>99%)**. Also provides ongoing contraception for 5–12 years. No hormones. May cause heavier periods. Best option if you were considering an IUD anyway. |

### Awareness in Kenya

💡 A study in Nairobi found that although EC (Postinor-2) is registered in Kenya, it was scarcely known or used. Awareness has grown since, but many women and girls still do not know about EC or where to access it. **It is available over-the-counter at most pharmacies without a prescription (levonorgestrel).** The sooner you act, the more effective it will be — do not wait.

---

### EC and HIV Risk

⚠️ Emergency contraception does NOT protect against HIV or other STIs. If you had unprotected sex with a partner whose HIV status you don't know, you may also need **PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis)** — which must be started within **72 hours** of exposure. Go to a hospital immediately and ask about PEP.
