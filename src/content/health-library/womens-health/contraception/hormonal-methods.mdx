## Hormonal Methods — How They Work

*Hormonal methods use synthetic hormones to prevent pregnancy — primarily by stopping ovulation. They come in various forms to suit different lifestyles and needs. None protect against STIs.*

---

### 1. Combined Oral Contraceptive Pill (COC / "The Pill")

Contains estrogen + progestin. Works by:
- **Preventing ovulation** (primary mechanism)
- Thickening cervical mucus (blocks sperm)
- Thinning the uterine lining

**Perfect use:** >99% effective  
**Typical use:** ~91% (up to 7 in 100 users pregnant in first year)

A standard pack has 21 active pills, then a 7-day break with a withdrawal bleed. If started on Day 1 of period: immediate protection. Any other day: use condoms for 7 days.

**✅ Pros:** Makes periods regular, lighter, less painful; reduces risk of ovarian, uterine and colorectal cancers; improves acne; fertility returns immediately after stopping.

**❌ Cons:** Must be taken same time every day; doesn't protect against STIs; some medications (HIV, TB, epilepsy drugs) reduce effectiveness; not suitable for smokers over 35, high blood pressure, or blood clot history.

**🚨 See a doctor if:** Severe leg pain/swelling (possible clot); chest pain or shortness of breath; severe headaches or vision changes; yellowing of skin/eyes; if you missed 2+ pills and had unprotected sex.

---

### 2. Progestogen-Only Pill (POP / Mini-Pill)

Contains only progestogen — works primarily by thickening cervical mucus. Some types (containing desogestrel) also stop ovulation.

**Critical difference:** Must be taken at the *same time every day* — the "missed pill" window is only **3–12 hours** depending on the type (vs 24 hours for the COC). Set a daily alarm.

**Best for:** Women who cannot take estrogen; breastfeeding mothers (safe immediately after birth); women with migraines with aura or high blood pressure.

**✅ Pros:** Safe while breastfeeding; can start immediately after childbirth, abortion or miscarriage; fertility returns immediately after stopping; suitable when estrogen is contraindicated.

**❌ Cons:** Irregular bleeding very common; requires strict timing; some medications interfere with effectiveness.

**If late:** If more than 3 hours late (or 12 hours for desogestrel pills) — follow missed pill instructions on package and use condoms for 2 days.

---

### 3. Contraceptive Injection (Depo-Provera / Sayana Press)

A progestin injection into the muscle (buttock or arm) — given every **3 months** by a healthcare provider. Works primarily by stopping ovulation.

**Effectiveness:** 94–99%  
**Kenya cost:** ~KSh 100 per injection at public facilities

One of the most popular methods in Kenya — private, convenient, only needed 4 times a year.

**✅ Pros:** Highly effective; private; periods often become lighter or stop (benefit for many); safe while breastfeeding.

**❌ Cons:** Requires clinic visit every 3 months; delay in return of fertility after stopping (may take several months to over a year); possible weight gain; irregular bleeding early on.

**Mark your calendar:** If more than 2 weeks late for your next injection, you may need pregnancy testing and backup contraception. Bone density may decrease with very long-term use — reversible after stopping.

---

### 4. Contraceptive Implant (Implanon / Jadelle)

A small, thin, flexible rod (matchstick-sized) inserted under the skin of the upper arm by a trained provider. Releases progestin steadily. Lasts **3–5 years** depending on type.

**Effectiveness: >99%** — one of the most reliable methods available

**"Fit and forget"** — once inserted, you don't have to think about it for years, yet it is fully reversible. Fertility returns quickly after removal. Not visible to others.

**✅ Pros:** >99% effective; long-acting; reversible; discreet; safe for those who cannot use estrogen; LARC (Long-Acting Reversible Contraception).

**❌ Cons:** Irregular bleeding is very common (most common side effect — periods may become lighter, heavier, longer or stop); some HIV/TB/epilepsy medications reduce effectiveness; requires trained provider.

**Kenya cost:** ~KSh 300 for insertion/removal at public facilities like Kiambu Level 5.

---

### 5. Contraceptive Patch & Vaginal Ring

**The Patch**

A small beige sticky patch releasing estrogen + progestin through the skin. Applied weekly for 3 weeks, then 1 patch-free week. **~91% effective with typical use**.

✅ Only need to remember it weekly. ❌ Less effective in women over 90 kg; may be visible; less widely available in Kenya.

Shares the same benefits and risks as the combined pill (including small blood clot risk).

**The Vaginal Ring (NuvaRing)**

A small flexible plastic ring inserted into the vagina for 3 weeks, then removed for 1 week. Releases estrogen + progestin. **~91% typical use, >97% perfect use**.

✅ Monthly convenience; user-controlled; lower systemic side effects for some women. ❌ Requires comfort with self-insertion; not widely available in Kenya.

If ring falls out: rinse with cool water and reinsert within 3 hours to maintain protection.
