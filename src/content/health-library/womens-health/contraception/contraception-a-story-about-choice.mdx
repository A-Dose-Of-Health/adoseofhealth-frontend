## Contraception: A Story About Choice

Contraception (family planning) allows you to prevent pregnancy and plan your family. Kenya has a wide range of methods available — from short-term to permanent. The "best" method is the one that works for your body, your lifestyle, your relationship and your future plans. There is no one-size-fits-all.

- **57%** — Married Kenyan women using a modern contraceptive method (KDHS 2022) — up from 32% two decades ago
- **14%** — Unmet need for contraception (women wanting to delay/stop childbearing but not using contraception) — down from 27% in 2003
- **88%** — Kenyan women now delivering in a health facility — a major improvement in maternal safety

💡 **The Golden Rule**

Only **condoms** protect against both pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV. If you are at risk of STIs, use condoms even if you are using another method for pregnancy prevention. This is called **dual method use**.

## Myth-Busting: Contraception in Kenya

| ❌ Myth | ✅ Truth |
|--------|---------|
| "Contraceptives cause infertility" | Modern contraceptives do not cause infertility. For most methods, fertility returns immediately or within a few months after stopping. The only exception is sterilisation, which is intended to be permanent. |
| "If you're breastfeeding, you can't get pregnant" | Only true if ALL three LAM conditions are met: exclusively breastfeeding, baby under 6 months, AND periods have not returned. If any condition is missing, you need additional contraception. |
| "Contraceptives cause cancer" | The combined pill slightly increases breast and cervical cancer risk, but also significantly reduces ovarian, endometrial and colorectal cancer risk. For most women, benefits outweigh risks. Discuss your personal risk with a provider. |
| "Women who use contraceptives are promiscuous" | Women use contraception for many valid reasons: to space children, complete education, protect health, plan families. Contraception is a tool for empowerment, not a moral judgement. |
| "Emergency contraception is the abortion pill" | EC prevents pregnancy by delaying ovulation — it does not work if a woman is already pregnant. The abortion pill (mifepristone) ends an existing pregnancy. They are completely different medications. |
| "Men won't agree to family planning" | Many men in Kenya support family planning when they understand its benefits. Engaging men as partners — through education, counselling and couples' visits — improves outcomes for everyone. |
| "Long-acting methods are not suitable for young women or those who haven't had children" | LARCs like implants and IUDs are safe and recommended for most women, including adolescents and nulliparous women. They are among the most effective methods available and fully reversible. |
