## Endometriosis

*Endometriosis is a condition where tissue similar to the uterine lining grows outside the uterus — on the ovaries, fallopian tubes, bowel, bladder, and other pelvic structures. This misplaced tissue responds to hormonal cycles: thickening, breaking down and bleeding with each period, but with no way to exit the body. This causes inflammation, scarring, adhesions and chronic pain. It is one of the leading causes of infertility.*

---

### Key Symptoms of Endometriosis

- **Severe, debilitating period pain** (dysmenorrhea) that OTC medication doesn't help
- **Chronic pelvic pain** even when not on your period
- **Pain during or after sex** (deep dyspareunia)
- Heavy menstrual bleeding or bleeding between periods
- Pain when urinating or having a bowel movement, especially during your period
- Fatigue
- **Difficulty conceiving (infertility)**
- Mood disorders and anxiety
- If in the chest: shortness of breath, coughing blood

---

### Diagnosis & The 4–12 Year Delay

The average time to diagnose endometriosis is **4–12 years** — because symptoms are often dismissed as "normal period pain."

**Investigations:**
- Pelvic exam — may feel cysts or scars behind the uterus
- Transvaginal ultrasound — can identify endometriomas ("chocolate cysts") on ovaries
- MRI — detailed images of deeper lesions
- **Laparoscopy** — minimally invasive surgery; the gold standard for definitive diagnosis. Allows direct visualisation and biopsy of lesions.

---

### Treatment Options for Endometriosis

There is no cure for endometriosis, but treatment can manage symptoms.

| Treatment | What It Does | Notes |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| **NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen)** | Reduce mild pain by lowering prostaglandins | First-line for pain management; often insufficient alone for severe endometriosis |
| **Combined contraceptive pill** | Suppresses the hormonal cycle that drives growth and bleeding of lesions | Must be stopped if trying to conceive — discuss timing with provider |
| **Mirena IUD (hormonal coil)** | Progestin locally thins the uterine lining; reduces pain and bleeding | Also provides contraception; fertility returns upon removal |
| **GnRH agonists** | Temporarily induce menopause by suppressing estrogen, shrinking lesions | Usually short-term (max 6 months) — can cause bone loss and menopausal symptoms |
| **Laparoscopic excision** | Surgical removal ("cutting out") of lesions — significant pain relief and improved fertility | Can be combined with treatment of endometriomas; may need repeat procedures |
| **IVF / Fertility treatment** | Assisted fertility methods if endometriosis affects conception | Discuss timing carefully — surgery may improve IVF outcomes in some cases |
