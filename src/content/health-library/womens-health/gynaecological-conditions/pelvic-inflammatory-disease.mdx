## Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)

*PID is an infection of the female upper reproductive tract — the uterus, fallopian tubes and ovaries. Usually caused by STIs like chlamydia and gonorrhoea, but can also result from other vaginal bacteria ascending upward. Many people with PID have no symptoms or only mild symptoms — and don't realise they have an infection until they have trouble conceiving.*

---

### Symptoms of Pelvic Inflammatory Disease

- Lower abdominal pain (dull or cramping between lower tummy and thighs)
- Fever (sometimes)
- Unusual vaginal discharge (yellow/green, foul-smelling)
- Pain felt deep inside during sex
- Painful and/or heavy periods (unusual for you)
- Vaginal bleeding between periods or after sex

---

### Risk Factors

- Sexually active women under 25 are at highest risk
- Multiple partners or a partner with multiple partners
- Prior history of PID or STIs
- Having recently given birth, had a miscarriage, or had a termination
- Having had an IUD fitted, hysteroscopy or any procedure that opens the cervix

**Treatment:** Course of 2+ antibiotics covering multiple bacteria — often started immediately before test results return. Partner must also be treated to prevent reinfection.

---

### Complications of Untreated PID

| Complication | What It Means |
|-------------|--------------|
| Tubo-ovarian abscess | Pus-filled swellings in the fallopian tubes or ovaries — can be life-threatening |
| Chronic pelvic pain | Long-term pain from scarring and adhesions in the pelvis |
| Tubal factor infertility | Scarring and blocking of tubes prevents egg and sperm meeting — accounts for up to 1/3 of infertility cases |
| Ectopic pregnancy | Damaged tubes increase risk of pregnancy implanting in the tube instead of the uterus — a medical emergency |

---

### Go to Hospital Immediately If

⚠️ Seek emergency care for any of the following:

- Severe pelvic pain getting worse or hurting when you move or touch the area
- Pelvic pain with heavy vaginal bleeding
- Sudden, sharp intense pain in lower abdomen
- Pain under the ribs on the right side and in the right shoulder (may indicate spread)
- Feeling dizzy or faint
