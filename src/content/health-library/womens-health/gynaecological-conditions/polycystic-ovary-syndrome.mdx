## Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)

*PCOS is a hormonal disorder affecting how the ovaries work. It is one of the most common hormonal conditions in women of reproductive age — and it is both a reproductive and metabolic condition. It increases the long-term risk of type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease.*

---

### The Rotterdam Criteria — Diagnosis Needs 2 of 3

1. **Irregular or absent ovulation** (irregular periods)
2. **Clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism** (excess hair growth, acne, or elevated testosterone on blood tests)
3. **Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound** (12+ follicles in one ovary OR ovarian volume >10 mL)

*Note: Despite the name, you do not actually have "cysts" — the follicles are underdeveloped sacs in which eggs develop but cannot mature and release.*

---

### Key Symptoms of Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

- Irregular or absent periods (oligomenorrhea or amenorrhea)
- Difficulty conceiving (infertility due to lack of ovulation)
- Excess facial or body hair (hirsutism)
- Acne (especially lower face, chest, upper back)
- Weight gain or difficulty losing weight, especially around the abdomen
- Thinning hair on the scalp (male-pattern baldness)
- Darkening of skin in folds (acanthosis nigricans) — neck, armpits, groin

---

### Why Insulin Resistance Matters in PCOS

Many women with PCOS are resistant to insulin — their body produces extra amounts to compensate. High insulin stimulates the ovaries to produce more testosterone, disrupting normal ovulation and contributing to symptoms. Insulin resistance can also lead to weight gain, which further worsens the cycle by increasing insulin levels.

---

### Treatment Options for Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

| Goal | Treatment | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| **First-line (all)** | Lifestyle modifications — as little as 5–10% weight loss can restore ovulation. Healthy diet, 150 min exercise/week, stress management, 6–8 hrs sleep. | Most effective foundation for ALL PCOS management |
| **Irregular periods** | Combined contraceptive pill or intermittent progestogen — regulates periods and reduces long-term endometrial cancer risk | Mirena IUS can also thin the lining without triggering regular bleeding |
| **Fertility** | Clomifene (first-line); letrozole; metformin (improves insulin resistance + ovulation); injectable gonadotrophins; IVF | Metformin is NOT a weight loss drug — it targets insulin resistance specifically |
| **Excess hair/acne** | Combined pill; eflornithine cream (facial hair); anti-androgens (spironolactone — not in pregnancy); laser removal | Anti-androgens require contraception as they can harm a male fetus |
| **Metabolic health** | GLP-1 agonists (Wegovy, Ozempic) for weight; cholesterol-lowering drugs; diabetes screening and management | Regular screening for diabetes and cardiovascular risk is essential |
| **Surgery (fertility only)** | Laparoscopic ovarian drilling (LOD) — minor procedure reducing androgen-producing tissue in ovaries to restore ovulation | Option when medications fail; lower multiple pregnancy risk than gonadotrophins |

🇰🇪 **Kenya Research Gap**

There are currently **no Kenyan studies on the prevalence of PCOS** in any population — meaning we have no data on how common it is in Kenya specifically. Given that up to 70% of global PCOS cases go undiagnosed, the true burden in Kenya is unknown. This is an area urgently needing research investment.
