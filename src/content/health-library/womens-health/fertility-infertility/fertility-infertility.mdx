# Fertility & Infertility

## Understanding Fertility & Conception

Fertility and contraception are two sides of the same coin. For some, the goal is to achieve pregnancy and build a family. For others, the goal is to prevent pregnancy and plan their lives. Both goals are valid and both require accurate information and access to quality healthcare. In Kenya, your right to reproductive health is protected by **Article 43 of the Constitution**, which guarantees the right to the highest attainable standard of health, including reproductive health.

- **3.1** — Kenya's total fertility rate in 2026 (down from 3.4 a decade ago)
- **1 in 6** — couples globally experience infertility at some point
- **10–15%** — of Kenyan couples unable to have a child

---

## The Fertile Window & Ovulation Signs

*For pregnancy to occur, sperm must meet an egg during the fertile window — the days leading up to and including ovulation. The egg lives only **12–24 hours** after release. Sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract for up to **5 days**. This means the fertile window is approximately **6 days long**: the 5 days before ovulation plus the day of ovulation itself.*

| Sign | What It Looks Like | How to Track |
|------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Cervical mucus changes** *(Most reliable sign)* | Clear, slippery, stretchy mucus like raw egg white ("EWCM") — the most fertile sign. Earlier in cycle: dry or thick white. After ovulation: thicker, cloudy or absent. | Check daily with toilet paper or clean fingers. The egg-white phase = peak fertility window. |
| **Basal body temperature (BBT)** | Temperature rises slightly 0.3–0.6°C *after* ovulation due to progesterone. Stays elevated throughout the luteal phase until next period. *Confirms ovulation has occurred — does not predict it.* | Take temperature every morning before getting out of bed with a BBT thermometer. Track on a chart or app. |
| **Ovulation pain (Mittelschmerz)** | Mild twinge or cramp on one side of the lower abdomen — lasts minutes to a few hours. The side alternates each month. | Note in diary or app. Not all women feel this and its absence does not mean no ovulation. |
| **Ovulation predictor kits (OPKs)** | Detect the LH surge that happens **24–36 hours before ovulation** — predicts your most fertile days before the egg is released. | Available at pharmacies. Urine test similar to a pregnancy test. Begin testing a few days before your expected ovulation. |

🇰🇪 **Kenya Research — Fertility Awareness in HIV-Serodifferent Couples**

A study in Thika, Kenya found that among women in HIV-serodifferent couples using fertility awareness methods, reporting "sticky" cervical mucus and a positive OPK were significantly associated with having condomless sex during peak fertility days. This confirms that FAMs can be effective tools for couples trying to conceive while managing HIV risk.

## Fertility Across Life Stages

| Age Group | Fertility Status & Monthly Chance | Key Considerations |
|-----------|----------------------------------|-------------------|
| **Teens (15–19)** | Highly fertile, but cycles may be irregular for first 1–2 years after menarche | High risk of unplanned pregnancy; need for contraception education and access |
| **20s** | Peak fertility years — monthly chance of pregnancy **20–25%** | Best time for childbearing biologically. Egg quality and quantity are at their peak. |
| **Early 30s** | Fertility begins gradual decline — monthly chance **15–20%** | Decline is slow. Most women in their early 30s conceive without difficulty. |
| **Late 30s (35–39)** | More noticeable decline — monthly chance **10–15%** | Increased risk of miscarriage and chromosomal abnormalities (egg quality declines). Seek help after 6 months of trying. |
| **40–44** | Low fertility — monthly chance **&lt;5%** | Many women in this age group require fertility assistance. Seek help immediately — do not wait. |
| **45+** | Very low — natural conception rare | Donor eggs often needed for pregnancy. IVF success rates drop significantly with own eggs. |

💡 **When to Seek Fertility Help**

- **Under 35:** After **1 year** of regular, unprotected sex without conceiving.
- **Ages 35–40:** After **6 months** of regular, unprotected sex without conceiving.
- **Over 40:** **Immediately — do not wait.**

Both partners should be evaluated. Infertility affects men and women equally — about one-third of cases involve female factors, one-third male factors, and one-third both or unexplained.

## Infertility — Causes & The Diagnostic Journey

*Infertility is defined as the inability to conceive after 12 months of regular, unprotected sex (6 months if over 35). It is a disease of the reproductive system — deserving the same compassion and evidence-based care as any other health issue. In Kenya, studies suggest 10–15% of couples are unable to have a child. Male factor contributes to 20–70% of infertility cases globally — in Kenya, it's estimated that in **60% of couples struggling to conceive**, the problem lies wholly or partly with the male partner.*

---

### Female Factor Infertility — Main Causes

| Category | Specific Causes | Notes |
|----------|---------------|-------|
| **Ovulation Disorders** | PCOS (most common); hypothalamic dysfunction (stress, low body weight, excessive exercise); Premature Ovarian Insufficiency (&lt;40); thyroid disorders; high prolactin | Problems with ovulation are a leading cause of female infertility — often hormonal and treatable |
| **Tubal Infertility** | Damage or blockage from PID (untreated STIs — chlamydia, gonorrhoea); previous abdominal or pelvic surgery; prior ectopic pregnancy; tubal ligation (intentional) | Accounts for up to one-third of female infertility cases. Often no symptoms — silent damage from untreated STIs. |
| **Uterine/Cervical Causes** | Fibroids (interfere with implantation); endometriosis (inflammation, scarring); uterine polyps or adhesions (Asherman's syndrome); congenital structural abnormalities (bicornuate uterus, uterine didelphys) | Many uterine causes are treatable — surgery can often restore fertility |

---

### Male Factor Infertility — Main Causes

Male infertility most often relates to problems with sperm: low count (**oligospermia**), poor movement (**poor motility**), or abnormal shape (**poor morphology**).

| Category | Causes |
|----------|-------|
| **Pre-testicular (Hormonal)** | Issues with hypothalamus or pituitary failing to signal testicles correctly — hormone imbalances, certain medications, tumours |
| **Testicular (Sperm production)** | Varicocele (enlarged scrotal veins, overheating testicles — seen in ~20% of infertile men); genetic defects (Klinefelter syndrome); infections (mumps after puberty, STIs causing scarring); undescended testicles in childhood; cancer treatment (chemotherapy, radiation) |
| **Post-testicular (Sperm delivery)** | Blocked vas deferens (infection, surgery, vasectomy); ejaculatory dysfunction (retrograde ejaculation); azoospermia (no sperm in semen — if production problem vs blockage determines treatability) |

✨ **Do NOT Let Anyone Tell You "You Are the Problem" Without Testing**

In Kenya, the burden of infertility has been unfairly placed on women. A **simple semen analysis** is the first and most important test for a man — painless, non-invasive and inexpensive. It should always be done alongside female investigations. In many cases, male infertility is treatable with simple lifestyle changes, medications or minor surgery.

## Lifestyle Risk Factors Affecting Both Partners

**Modifiable Risk Factors**

- **Age** — female fertility declines significantly after 35; male declines gradually
- **Smoking** — harms sperm quality and accelerates egg loss
- **Excess alcohol** — impairs sperm production and ovulation
- **Recreational drugs** (cannabis, cocaine) — affect fertility in both sexes
- **Weight** — significantly underweight or overweight disrupts ovulation and sperm quality
- **Stress** — interferes with hormones regulating reproduction
- **Environmental toxins** — pesticides, lead and industrial chemicals

---

**Male-Specific Tips**

- Avoid **tight underwear** — increases scrotal temperature, temporarily impairing sperm production
- Take breaks from **prolonged sitting** (long-distance driving, desk work) — same reason
- Avoid **hot baths and saunas** frequently
- Even small changes help — switching from tight briefs to loose boxers can improve sperm quality over time

*Sperm production takes ~74 days — lifestyle changes take time to show results.*

## Diagnostic Tests

| Test | For Whom | What It Shows |
|------|---------|--------------|
| **Semen analysis** | Male partner — always done first | Sperm count (normal: >15M/mL), motility, morphology, volume. If abnormal, repeat — sperm quality fluctuates. |
| **Ovulation testing** | Female — Day 21 progesterone blood test | Confirms whether ovulation occurred in that cycle |
| **AMH (Anti-Müllerian Hormone)** | Female — any cycle day | Estimates ovarian reserve (egg quantity remaining). Low AMH = reduced reserve. Does not perfectly predict pregnancy. |
| **Day-3 FSH & estradiol** | Female — day 3 of cycle | Elevated FSH suggests declining ovarian reserve — pituitary working harder to stimulate ovaries |
| **HSG (Hysterosalpingography)** | Female — X-ray with dye | Checks if fallopian tubes are open and uterine cavity is normal shape. Key fertility test. |
| **Pelvic ultrasound** | Female | Examines uterus, ovaries; antral follicle count (indicator of ovarian reserve); checks for fibroids, polyps, cysts |
| **Hysteroscopy** | Female | Camera directly visualises inside the uterus — finds and treats polyps, fibroids, adhesions |
| **STI screening** | Both partners | Chlamydia and gonorrhoea — untreated STIs cause tubal damage and infertility |
| **Genetic testing** | Both — if suspected genetic cause | Very low/zero sperm count, family history of genetic conditions, recurrent miscarriage |

## Fertility Treatment Options

### 1. Lifestyle Changes & Timed Intercourse

For some couples, simple changes — quitting smoking, reaching a healthy weight, reducing alcohol, managing stress, and timing sex to the fertile window using OPKs or FAMs — are all that is needed. Never underestimate the power of lifestyle. This is always the first step.

---

### 2. Medical Treatments

| Medication | What It Does | For Whom |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| **Clomifene citrate** | Stimulates ovulation — the first-line drug for women who don't ovulate regularly | PCOS and other ovulatory disorders; taken days 2–6 of cycle |
| **Letrozole** | Also stimulates ovulation — increasingly preferred over clomifene in PCOS | May have better ovulation and pregnancy rates in PCOS than clomifene; lower multiple pregnancy risk |
| **Metformin** | Improves insulin resistance, encourages ovulation in PCOS | PCOS with insulin resistance — often combined with clomifene. NOT a weight loss drug. |
| **Injectable gonadotrophins (FSH/LH)** | Directly stimulate follicle development in the ovaries | When oral drugs fail — requires close monitoring; higher multiple pregnancy risk |
| **Antibiotics** | Treat underlying infections affecting fertility in either partner | If STI or reproductive tract infection identified on screening |

⚠️ **About Fertility Drugs**

Fertility drugs can have side effects including hot flashes, mood swings and — with injectable gonadotrophins — an increased risk of multiple pregnancy (twins, triplets). Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) is a risk with injectable medications. Always take them under close medical supervision with ultrasound monitoring.

---

### 3. Surgical Treatments

| Procedure | What It Does | Notes |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| **Myomectomy** | Surgical removal of fibroids, preserving the uterus and fertility | Fibroids can regrow — discuss timing relative to fertility plans |
| **Polypectomy (hysteroscopic)** | Removal of endometrial polyps that may interfere with implantation | Simple procedure — day case; often dramatically improves IVF outcomes |
| **Laparoscopic endometriosis treatment** | Excision of endometriosis lesions; drainage of endometriomas | May improve natural conception and IVF success in selected cases |
| **Laparoscopic ovarian drilling (LOD)** | Minor procedure reducing androgen-producing tissue in ovaries to restore ovulation in PCOS | Option when medications fail; lower multiple pregnancy risk than gonadotrophins |
| **Varicocelectomy (male)** | Surgical repair of varicocele — can improve sperm quality | Varicocele found in ~20% of infertile men; surgery improves counts in many |
| **Vasectomy reversal** | Restores sperm to the semen after vasectomy | 75–90% success rate in restoring sperm; pregnancy rates depend on time since vasectomy and partner age |

---

### 4. Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART)

| Procedure | How It Works | Success Rate & Notes |
|-----------|------------|---------------------|
| **IUI — Intrauterine Insemination** | "Washed" and concentrated sperm placed directly into the uterus around ovulation. Often combined with ovulation-stimulating drugs. Less invasive than IVF. | ~7–8% pregnancy rate per cycle. Useful for: mild male factor infertility, cervical mucus problems, unexplained infertility, donor sperm use. |
| **IVF — In Vitro Fertilisation** | Eggs retrieved from ovaries and fertilised with sperm in a laboratory. Resulting embryo(s) transferred back into the uterus. Previously known as "test tube baby." | Success rates vary by age: ~40% per cycle under 35; drops significantly after 40. Used for: blocked tubes, severe male factor, endometriosis, unexplained infertility. |
| **ICSI — Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection** | A single healthy sperm injected directly into an egg under a microscope. Sperm can even be surgically retrieved from the testicle (TESA/PESA) if not in semen. | ~20–30% pregnancy rate per cycle. A game-changer for severe male factor infertility — even men with very few or no sperm in semen may father children. |
| **Donor Insemination (DI)** | Using carefully screened donor sperm to inseminate the woman | Option if male partner has no sperm and the condition is untreatable. Legal in Kenya under the new ART Act. |
| **Egg/Embryo Donation** | Using donor eggs (fertilised with partner's or donor sperm) for women whose own eggs cannot be used | Option for women with premature ovarian insufficiency, very poor egg quality, or genetic conditions. Now regulated under the ART Act. |
| **Fertility Preservation** | Egg or sperm freezing for those facing medical treatments (chemotherapy, radiation) that could affect fertility, or for those wishing to delay childbearing | Egg freezing is most effective under age 35. Now legally recognised in Kenya. |

## Kenya's Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART) Act, 2022

*In November 2025, the National Assembly passed the Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART) Bill, 2022 — a landmark law providing for the first time a comprehensive legal and regulatory framework for fertility care in Kenya. This places Kenya among a handful of African nations with such a forward-thinking framework.*

| Provision | What It Means for Kenyans |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Legalises and regulates fertility treatment** | IVF, IUI, gamete (egg/sperm) donation, embryo donation and surrogacy are now legally recognised and will be regulated for ethical, safe practice |
| **Establishes an ART Committee** | Under the Kenya Medical Practitioners and Dentists Council (KMPDC) — will license clinics, maintain a confidential national register of donors, embryos and children conceived through ART |
| **Eligibility criteria** | Intended parents: Kenyan citizens aged 25–55 years. Surrogate mothers: Kenyan citizens aged 25–45 years, must have borne at least one child and pass medical and psychological assessments. |
| **Prohibits commercial surrogacy** | Only altruistic surrogacy is permitted — surrogates can receive medical expenses but no payment, preventing exploitation |
| **Criminalises unethical practices** | Human cloning, sale of gametes/embryos and sex selection are banned — penalties up to KSh 10 million fine or 10 years imprisonment |
| **Parental leave provisions** | Surrogates: 3 months' leave after birth. Intended mothers and fathers receive maternity and paternity leave respectively. |

⚠️ **Current Status**

The law is **awaiting Senate concurrence and Presidential assent** to become fully operational. Once enacted, it will transform fertility care in Kenya — providing legal clarity, ethical standards and protection for all parties involved in fertility treatment.

## Safer Conception — Special Considerations for HIV

*Kenya has a significant HIV burden, and many couples include one HIV-positive and one HIV-negative partner (serodifferent couples). These couples face a unique challenge: wanting to achieve pregnancy while minimising HIV transmission risk to the negative partner and the baby. Modern strategies make this achievable.*

| Strategy | How It Works | Key Facts |
|----------|------------|----------|
| **Treatment as Prevention (TasP)** | HIV-positive partner takes antiretrovirals consistently to achieve viral suppression | When viral load is **undetectable, risk of transmission is effectively zero** — U=U (Undetectable = Untransmissible) |
| **PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis)** | HIV-negative partner takes daily pill OR injectable lenacapavir (Sunlenca) every 6 months to prevent HIV acquisition | Daily oral PrEP reduces risk by up to 95%. Injectable lenacapavir (twice yearly) is now available as an alternative. |
| **Timed condomless sex** | Using fertility awareness methods to identify the fertile window — having condomless sex only during those peak days | A Thika study confirmed FAM-based timing can align condomless sex with peak fertility. Antiretroviral-based strategies remain essential alongside this. |
| **Self-insemination** | Partner's sperm used for at-home insemination during the fertile window — avoids HIV exposure entirely | Can be done without clinical assistance; avoids any sexual transmission risk |
| **Sperm washing + IVF/IUI** | Sperm is "washed" to eliminate the virus, then used for IVF or IUI | Available at specialised fertility clinics; expensive but highly effective at eliminating risk |

💡 **ePrEP — Innovative Access in Kenya**

An innovative ePrEP model is making HIV prevention more accessible in Kenya. Through partnerships with online pharmacies like **MyDAWA**, individuals can access PrEP and other prevention tools privately and conveniently. As Dr. Daniel Were of Jhpiego explains, bundling emergency contraception with HIV preventive tools ensures holistic care is addressed.

## Coping with Infertility — Emotional Support

**Strategies That Help**

1. **Talk to each other** — infertility can strain a relationship; keep communication open and honest
2. **Seek counselling** — a counsellor specialising in infertility provides a safe space to process grief, anger and frustration
3. **Find support groups** — connecting with others on the same journey is validating; online communities and forums can provide support where formal groups are limited
4. **Set boundaries** — it is okay to protect your mental health by stepping back from baby showers or events that feel too painful
5. **Take breaks from treatment** — the emotional and financial toll can be overwhelming; it is okay to pause and focus on yourselves

---

**What to Remember**

- Infertility is a medical condition — **not a punishment, not a curse, not your fault**
- Your emotional health matters as much as your physical health
- Stress does contribute to hormonal disruption — prioritising mental health is medically important, not just a luxury
- Even with unexplained infertility, many couples go on to conceive — either naturally over time or with less intensive treatments
- **You are not alone** — millions of couples worldwide are on this same journey

*Seeking support is a sign of strength, not weakness. You deserve care for both your body and your heart.*

## Myth-Busting: Fertility in Kenya

| ❌ Myth | ✅ Truth |
|--------|---------|
| "Infertility is always the woman's problem" | Infertility affects men and women equally. In Kenya, up to 60% of couples struggling to conceive have a problem wholly or partly with the male partner. Both partners must be evaluated. |
| "If you haven't conceived after a few months, something is wrong" | Even for young, healthy couples, the chance of conceiving in any given month is only 20–25%. It can take up to a year of trying before pregnancy occurs — this is normal. Seek help after 1 year (6 months if over 35). |
| "IVF always works" | IVF success rates vary significantly with age: about 40% per cycle under 35, dropping to under 10% per cycle over 42 with own eggs. It often takes multiple cycles. Have realistic expectations. |
| "Traditional herbs can boost fertility" | No herbal remedy has been scientifically proven to restore fertility. Relying on herbs can delay diagnosis and proper treatment. Some herbs can interfere with medications. Always tell your provider about anything you are taking. |
| "You can tell you're fertile just by how regular your periods are" | Regular periods do not guarantee ovulation or fertility. Many women have regular cycles but do not ovulate every month (anovulation). The only way to confirm ovulation is through monitoring. |
