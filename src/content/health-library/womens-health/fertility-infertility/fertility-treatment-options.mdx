## Fertility Treatment Options

---

### 1. Lifestyle Changes & Timed Intercourse

For some couples, simple changes — quitting smoking, reaching a healthy weight, reducing alcohol, managing stress, and timing sex to the fertile window using OPKs or FAMs — are all that is needed. Never underestimate the power of lifestyle. This is always the first step.

---

### 2. Medical Treatments

| Medication | What It Does | For Whom |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| **Clomifene citrate** | Stimulates ovulation — the first-line drug for women who don't ovulate regularly | PCOS and other ovulatory disorders; taken days 2–6 of cycle |
| **Letrozole** | Also stimulates ovulation — increasingly preferred over clomifene in PCOS | May have better ovulation and pregnancy rates in PCOS than clomifene; lower multiple pregnancy risk |
| **Metformin** | Improves insulin resistance, encourages ovulation in PCOS | PCOS with insulin resistance — often combined with clomifene. NOT a weight loss drug. |
| **Injectable gonadotrophins (FSH/LH)** | Directly stimulate follicle development in the ovaries | When oral drugs fail — requires close monitoring; higher multiple pregnancy risk |
| **Antibiotics** | Treat underlying infections affecting fertility in either partner | If STI or reproductive tract infection identified on screening |

⚠️ **About Fertility Drugs**

Fertility drugs can have side effects including hot flashes, mood swings and — with injectable gonadotrophins — an increased risk of multiple pregnancy (twins, triplets). Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) is a risk with injectable medications. Always take them under close medical supervision with ultrasound monitoring.

---

### 3. Surgical Treatments

| Procedure | What It Does | Notes |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| **Myomectomy** | Surgical removal of fibroids, preserving the uterus and fertility | Fibroids can regrow — discuss timing relative to fertility plans |
| **Polypectomy (hysteroscopic)** | Removal of endometrial polyps that may interfere with implantation | Simple procedure — day case; often dramatically improves IVF outcomes |
| **Laparoscopic endometriosis treatment** | Excision of endometriosis lesions; drainage of endometriomas | May improve natural conception and IVF success in selected cases |
| **Laparoscopic ovarian drilling (LOD)** | Minor procedure reducing androgen-producing tissue in ovaries to restore ovulation in PCOS | Option when medications fail; lower multiple pregnancy risk than gonadotrophins |
| **Varicocelectomy (male)** | Surgical repair of varicocele — can improve sperm quality | Varicocele found in ~20% of infertile men; surgery improves counts in many |
| **Vasectomy reversal** | Restores sperm to the semen after vasectomy | 75–90% success rate in restoring sperm; pregnancy rates depend on time since vasectomy and partner age |

### 4. Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART)

| Procedure | How It Works | Success Rate & Notes |
|-----------|------------|---------------------|
| **IUI — Intrauterine Insemination** | "Washed" and concentrated sperm placed directly into the uterus around ovulation. Often combined with ovulation-stimulating drugs. Less invasive than IVF. | ~7–8% pregnancy rate per cycle. Useful for: mild male factor infertility, cervical mucus problems, unexplained infertility, donor sperm use. |
| **IVF — In Vitro Fertilisation** | Eggs retrieved from ovaries and fertilised with sperm in a laboratory. Resulting embryo(s) transferred back into the uterus. Previously known as "test tube baby." | Success rates vary by age: ~40% per cycle under 35; drops significantly after 40. Used for: blocked tubes, severe male factor, endometriosis, unexplained infertility. |
| **ICSI — Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection** | A single healthy sperm injected directly into an egg under a microscope. Sperm can even be surgically retrieved from the testicle (TESA/PESA) if not in semen. | ~20–30% pregnancy rate per cycle. A game-changer for severe male factor infertility — even men with very few or no sperm in semen may father children. |
| **Donor Insemination (DI)** | Using carefully screened donor sperm to inseminate the woman | Option if male partner has no sperm and the condition is untreatable. Legal in Kenya under the new ART Act. |
| **Egg/Embryo Donation** | Using donor eggs (fertilised with partner's or donor sperm) for women whose own eggs cannot be used | Option for women with premature ovarian insufficiency, very poor egg quality, or genetic conditions. Now regulated under the ART Act. |
| **Fertility Preservation** | Egg or sperm freezing for those facing medical treatments (chemotherapy, radiation) that could affect fertility, or for those wishing to delay childbearing | Egg freezing is most effective under age 35. Now legally recognised in Kenya. |

## Kenya's Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART) Act, 2022

*In November 2025, the National Assembly passed the Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART) Bill, 2022 — a landmark law providing for the first time a comprehensive legal and regulatory framework for fertility care in Kenya. This places Kenya among a handful of African nations with such a forward-thinking framework.*

| Provision | What It Means for Kenyans |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Legalises and regulates fertility treatment** | IVF, IUI, gamete (egg/sperm) donation, embryo donation and surrogacy are now legally recognised and will be regulated for ethical, safe practice |
| **Establishes an ART Committee** | Under the Kenya Medical Practitioners and Dentists Council (KMPDC) — will license clinics, maintain a confidential national register of donors, embryos and children conceived through ART |
| **Eligibility criteria** | Intended parents: Kenyan citizens aged 25–55 years. Surrogate mothers: Kenyan citizens aged 25–45 years, must have borne at least one child and pass medical and psychological assessments. |
| **Prohibits commercial surrogacy** | Only altruistic surrogacy is permitted — surrogates can receive medical expenses but no payment, preventing exploitation |
| **Criminalises unethical practices** | Human cloning, sale of gametes/embryos and sex selection are banned — penalties up to KSh 10 million fine or 10 years imprisonment |
| **Parental leave provisions** | Surrogates: 3 months' leave after birth. Intended mothers and fathers receive maternity and paternity leave respectively. |

⚠️ **Current Status**

The law is **awaiting Senate concurrence and Presidential assent** to become fully operational. Once enacted, it will transform fertility care in Kenya — providing legal clarity, ethical standards and protection for all parties involved in fertility treatment.

## Safer Conception — Special Considerations for HIV

*Kenya has a significant HIV burden, and many couples include one HIV-positive and one HIV-negative partner (serodifferent couples). These couples face a unique challenge: wanting to achieve pregnancy while minimising HIV transmission risk to the negative partner and the baby. Modern strategies make this achievable.*

| Strategy | How It Works | Key Facts |
|----------|------------|----------|
| **Treatment as Prevention (TasP)** | HIV-positive partner takes antiretrovirals consistently to achieve viral suppression | When viral load is **undetectable, risk of transmission is effectively zero** — U=U (Undetectable = Untransmissible) |
| **PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis)** | HIV-negative partner takes daily pill OR injectable lenacapavir (Sunlenca) every 6 months to prevent HIV acquisition | Daily oral PrEP reduces risk by up to 95%. Injectable lenacapavir (twice yearly) is now available as an alternative. |
| **Timed condomless sex** | Using fertility awareness methods to identify the fertile window — having condomless sex only during those peak days | A Thika study confirmed FAM-based timing can align condomless sex with peak fertility. Antiretroviral-based strategies remain essential alongside this. |
| **Self-insemination** | Partner's sperm used for at-home insemination during the fertile window — avoids HIV exposure entirely | Can be done without clinical assistance; avoids any sexual transmission risk |
| **Sperm washing + IVF/IUI** | Sperm is "washed" to eliminate the virus, then used for IVF or IUI | Available at specialised fertility clinics; expensive but highly effective at eliminating risk |

💡 **ePrEP — Innovative Access in Kenya**

An innovative ePrEP model is making HIV prevention more accessible in Kenya. Through partnerships with online pharmacies like **MyDAWA**, individuals can access PrEP and other prevention tools privately and conveniently. As Dr. Daniel Were of Jhpiego explains, bundling emergency contraception with HIV preventive tools ensures holistic care is addressed.
