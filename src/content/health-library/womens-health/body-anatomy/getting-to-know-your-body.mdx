<TlDr>
- Most women were never taught the correct names for their own anatomy.
- Body literacy — knowing how your body works — is a health skill, not a luxury.
- The language you use about your body affects how you communicate with healthcare providers and how quickly conditions get diagnosed.
- This subtopic covers external and internal anatomy in plain language, without shame.
</TlDr>

Even before puberty begins, it is important to know the names and functions of your body parts. This knowledge is not shameful, it is protective.

## 1. External Genitalia (The Vulva)
The vulva is the name for all the external female reproductive strucutres you can see. It includes:
<MdxTable title="An Overview Of The Structures and Functions of the External Female Genitalia">


| Structure | What It Is |
|---|---|
| Labia majora | The outer, fleshy lips that protect the inner structures |
| Labia minora | The inner, delicate lips that surround the openings |
| Clitoris | A small sensory organ at the top, rich in nerve endings; its only purpose is pleasure (yes you read correct) |
| Urethral opening | The tiny hole where urine comes out (if we are counting, it’s the first hole from the front) |
| Vaginal opening | The entrance to the vagina |

</MdxTable>
<Callout variant="tip" title="Key Point">
Every vulva is unique. There is no single "normal" shape, size, or colour. 

Variation is normal and beautiful.
</Callout>


## 2. Internal Organs
The internal reproductive organs include:
<MdxTable title="An Overview Of The Organs and Functions of The Internal Female Genitalia">


| Organ | What It Is |
|---|---|
| Vagina | The stretchy canal that connects the uterus to the outside |
| Cervix | The lower part of the uterus, opening into the vagina |
| Uterus (womb) | The hollow, pear-shaped organ where a baby grows |
| Ovaries | Two small organs that store eggs and produce hormones |
| Fallopian tubes | The pathways where egg meets sperm |

</MdxTable>
