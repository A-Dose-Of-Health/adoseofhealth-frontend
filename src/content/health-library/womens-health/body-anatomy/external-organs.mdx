## 1. What Is the Vulva?

The vulva is the collective name for all the female external genitalia. While many people use the word "vagina" to describe this area, the vagina is actually the internal canal. The vulva is what you can see on the outside.

### Mons Pubis

The fatty, rounded area above the pubic bone, covered with pubic hair after puberty. Provides cushioning during activities and protects the pubic bone. Pubic hair removal is a personal choice, not a medical necessity — shaving or waxing can increase the risk of irritation, ingrown hairs and folliculitis.

---

### Labia Majora (Outer Lips)

Two fleshy folds from the mons pubis to the perineum containing fatty tissue, sweat and oil glands. Outer surface is pigmented with pubic hair; inner surface is smooth. **Primary role:** protect the more delicate inner structures from friction, injury and pathogens. The female homologue of the scrotum. May shrink slightly after menopause as oestrogen declines.

---

### Labia Minora (Inner Lips)

Two thin, hairless folds inside the outer lips — highly variable in appearance. Made of **erectile tissue** (no fat), they swell and may darken during arousal. Densely packed with nerve endings. They split at the top to form the **clitoral hood (prepuce)** and frenulum; meet at the bottom at the posterior fourchette. Asymmetry between sides is completely normal and common.

---

### Clitoris (The Full Structure)

Much larger than the visible glans. It is a wishbone-shaped organ with:
- **Glans** (visible tip, ~8,000 nerve endings — more than any other human structure)
- **Body** (extends internally beneath the hood)
- **Crura/Legs** (two wings hugging the urethra and vagina internally)
- **Vestibular bulbs** Its only known purpose is pleasure. Develops from the same embryonic tissue as the penis.

---

### Urethral Opening (Meatus)

The tiny hole below the clitoris and above the vaginal opening where urine exits. **NOT** part of the reproductive system. Female urethra is only ~4 cm long (vs 20 cm in males) — this short distance explains why women are far more prone to UTIs. You cannot insert a tampon into the urethra — it is far too small. Urine and menstrual products exit through separate channels.

---

### Vaginal Opening & Hymen

The entrance to the vaginal canal, surrounded by the **hymen** — a thin, stretchy membrane that varies enormously between individuals (crescent, ring, fringed, or very small). It can stretch from exercise, tampon use or medical exams. Its presence does **NOT** indicate sexual history. Some people are born without a hymen. First penetration may or may not cause discomfort or bleeding — both are normal.

---

### Bartholin's Glands

Two pea-sized glands at the 4 o'clock and 8 o'clock positions near the vaginal opening. Not active until puberty. Produce lubrication during arousal. Usually unnoticed when healthy. Can become blocked forming a painless **cyst**, or infected forming a painful **abscess** (swollen, tender lump making sitting/walking difficult — requires drainage by a healthcare provider).

---

### Skene's Glands (Paraurethral)

Two small glands on either side of the lower end of the urethra, sometimes called the "female prostate" due to functional similarities. They produce fluid into the urethra during arousal, believed to contribute to lubrication and female ejaculation. Their tiny ducts are vulnerable to ascending bacteria — blockage or infection causes **skenitis**.

---

💡 **Key Point**

The vulva does not need special products to be "clean." Warm water and gentle care are all that is required. Less is more. Harsh soaps, douches, vaginal sprays and vaginal steaming cause irritation and disrupt natural balance — there is no evidence any of them benefit health.

## Vulva Hygiene: Daily Do's & Don'ts

### ✅ Do

- Wash the external vulva once daily with warm water only, or a very mild, fragrance-free pH-balanced intimate cleanser
- Wipe from front to back after using the toilet — this prevents E. coli reaching the urethra or vagina
- Pat gently dry with a clean soft towel (avoid rubbing)
- Wear breathable cotton underwear during the day; avoid wearing underwear to bed
- Change out of wet clothing (swimsuits, gym wear) promptly — moisture breeds yeast and bacteria
- Change sanitary products regularly as per instructions

---

### 🚫 Never

- Douche — it disrupts healthy bacteria and increases infection risk
- Use scented soaps, bubble baths, sprays or wipes on the vulva
- Use scented tampons or pads
- Attempt vaginal steaming — no proven benefits, risk of serious burns and flora disruption
- Wear tight, non-breathable fabrics for prolonged periods
- Flush used pads or tampons — wrap and bin them

## Common Vulvar Conditions

| Condition | Key Symptoms | Action |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **Vulvitis** | Redness, swelling, itching, burning — caused by irritants, infections or allergies | Identify and remove trigger; see provider if persistent |
| **Bartholin's Abscess** | Tender, hot, swollen painful lump near vaginal opening; makes sitting/walking difficult | See provider promptly — requires drainage |
| **Vulvodynia** | Chronic, unexplained burning, stinging, rawness or pain lasting ≥3 months with no identifiable cause | Requires specialist evaluation — treatable |
| **Contact Dermatitis** | Itching, burning, red rash — allergic or irritant reaction (soaps, detergents, lubricants) | Remove the irritant; antihistamines or corticosteroids if needed |
| **Genital Warts (HPV)** | Flesh-coloured, cauliflower-like bumps on vulva or perianal area | See provider — treatable; HPV vaccine prevents most cases |
| **Herpes** | Painful, fluid-filled blisters or open sores; tingling or burning before outbreak | See provider — antiviral treatment reduces outbreaks |
| **Lichen Sclerosus** | White, thin skin patches; intense itching; may cause scarring — increases vulvar cancer risk | Regular follow-up with gynaecologist essential |
