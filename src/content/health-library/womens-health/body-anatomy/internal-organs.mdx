# The Vagina — Internal Canal

*The vagina is a stretchy, self-cleaning fibromuscular canal connecting the cervix to the outside world. It serves menstruation, sexual function, conception and childbirth. It is not the same as the vulva — the vagina is purely internal.*

---

## The Three Layers of the Vaginal Wall

### 1. Mucosal Layer (Inner)

Similar to skin but without glands. Produces moisture via **transudation** (fluid seeping through walls). Contains **rugae** — transverse folds like pleats that allow dramatic stretching during sex and childbirth. Hosts **Lactobacillus** bacteria that produce lactic acid maintaining protective pH 3.8–4.5.

### 2. Muscular Layer (Middle)

Smooth muscle that contracts and expands. Helps expel discharge and menstrual fluid. Surrounded and supported by the pelvic floor muscles — pelvic floor health directly affects vaginal function.

### 3. Adventitia Layer (Outer)

Connective tissue rich in collagen and elastic fibres. Anchors the vagina to surrounding pelvic structures (bladder, rectum, urethra). Allows expansion and recoil. The slightly acidic discharge that can bleach dark underwear is completely normal — it's the vaginal flora doing their job.

---

💡 **The Vagina is Self-Cleaning**

The vaginal flora — healthy bacteria naturally living in the vagina — are your first line of defence. They maintain an acidic pH that prevents harmful bacteria and yeast from overgrowing. Douching, scented products and vaginal steaming disrupt this system. The vagina is like the eye — it cleans itself and needs no internal washing.

---

## Vaginal Infections

### The Three Main Types

| Infection | Cause | Key Signs | Treatment |
|-----------|-------|----------|-----------| 
| **Bacterial Vaginosis (BV)** *Most common cause of vaginal discharge* | Overgrowth of harmful bacteria, decrease in Lactobacilli. Triggered by douching, new/multiple sex partners, no condoms. **NOT an STI** but sexual activity can trigger it. | Thin, grey or white discharge; strong fishy odour (especially after sex); mild itching or burning | Prescription antibiotics (metronidazole or clindamycin). Partner does not need treatment for first episode. |
| **Yeast Infection (Candidiasis)** *Candida albicans overgrowth* | Overgrowth of Candida fungus. Risk factors: antibiotic use (kills healthy bacteria), high oestrogen (pregnancy, HRT), uncontrolled diabetes, weakened immune system (HIV/AIDS) | Thick, white, cottage-cheese-like discharge; intense itching; redness and swelling of vulva; burning during urination or sex | Over-the-counter antifungal creams or suppositories (if confirmed). See provider if first time, pregnant, or recurrent. |
| **Trichomoniasis ("Trich")** *STI — Trichomonas vaginalis parasite* | Sexually transmitted parasite. Many people have NO symptoms and unknowingly spread it. Both partners must be tested and treated. | Frothy, yellow-green discharge with strong odour; itching and redness; pain during urination or sex; lower abdominal discomfort | Prescription antibiotics (metronidazole). **Both partners must be treated** to prevent reinfection. |
| **Vaginal Atrophy (GSM)** *Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause* | Thinning, drying and inflammation of vaginal walls due to decreased oestrogen — most common during menopause or breastfeeding | Vaginal dryness, burning, decreased lubrication, pain during sex, urinary urgency, recurrent UTIs | Vaginal moisturizers (first-line), lubricants, low-dose vaginal oestrogen (prescription), ospemifene tablets |

---

⚠️ **Critical Warning**

Vaginal symptoms often overlap — yeast infection, BV and trichomoniasis can all cause itching and discharge but require completely different treatments. **Self-diagnosis is unreliable.** Do not use over-the-counter antifungal creams if you're not certain it's a yeast infection — using them for BV or trichomoniasis will not work and will delay proper treatment.

## Kenya Myth-Busting: Vaginal Health

| ❌ Myth | ✅ Truth |
|--------|---------|
| "The vagina must be washed inside to stay clean" | The vagina is self-cleaning. Its discharge and healthy bacteria keep it balanced. Internal washing (douching) removes good bacteria and increases infection risk. |
| "If there's no smell, there's no infection" | Many infections including chlamydia, gonorrhoea and early trichomoniasis often have NO symptoms. You cannot rely on smell or appearance alone. |
| "Pain during sex is normal for women" | Persistent pain during intercourse (dyspareunia) is a medical symptom, not a normal part of being a woman. It requires evaluation. |
| "Vaginal steaming cleanses the womb" | No evidence supports this. Steaming can cause serious burns to vaginal walls and disrupt healthy flora, leading to infections. |
| "The hymen determines virginity" | The hymen is a stretchy membrane with natural variation. It can be stretched by many non-sexual activities. It does not prove sexual history. |
| "Public toilets can give you an STI" | Most STI pathogens are fragile and cannot survive on toilet seats. They require close skin-to-skin or fluid contact for transmission. |

# The Cervix — The Gatekeeper

*The cervix is the lower, cylindrical part of the uterus that connects the uterine body to the vagina. Approximately 4 cm long and 3 cm in diameter. It is not a separate organ — it's an integral part of the uterus, acting as passageway, protective barrier and producer of critical fluids.*

---

## Four Critical Functions

**1. Gatekeeper for sperm:** Thick plug-like mucus most of the month; around ovulation becomes thin, clear and stretchy (egg-white) creating a sperm-friendly environment.

**2. Protective barrier:** Remains tightly closed during pregnancy.

**3. Childbirth pathway:** Softens, effaces and dilates to ~10 cm during labour.

**4. Menstrual passage:** Allows menstrual blood to exit.

---

## The Transformation Zone — Why It Matters

The cervix has two cell types: **Glandular cells** (columnar, line the canal, produce mucus — reddish velvet appearance) and **Squamous cells** (flat, protective, cover the ectocervix — smooth pink). Where they meet is the **transformation zone (squamocolumnar junction)** — the most common site for cervical cancer to develop. This is why Pap smears target this area specifically. Its location shifts with age and hormones.

✨ **Cervix Changes You Can Track**

The cervix changes texture and position throughout your menstrual cycle. Around ovulation it becomes **softer, higher and more open** (like pursed lips) to allow sperm entry. After ovulation it becomes **firmer, lower and closed**. Tracking these changes is a valid form of fertility awareness.

---

## Cervical Screening — Kenya Guidelines

| Test | What It Detects | When & How Often |
|------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Pap Smear** | Abnormal cell changes on the cervix that could potentially become cancer | Ages 21–25: every 3 years; Ages 25–65: every 3 years (preferred) or co-test with HPV every 5 years |
| **HPV Test** | High-risk HPV types (especially 16 & 18) that cause cell changes | Ages 26–65: every 5 years (preferred method); can be self-collected |
| **Co-testing** | Both HPV + Pap together | Every 5 years, ages 25–65 |
| **HIV-positive women** | Pap smear every year from age 21 | Annual — HIV increases cervical cancer risk significantly |

🇰🇪 **Kenya Context**

Kenya transitioned to a **single-dose HPV vaccination schedule in November 2025**, making it easier to reach more girls. The HPV vaccine is free of charge at public health facilities for girls aged 10 years. Research by Kenyan doctors — Dr. Nelly Mugo, Dr. Elizabeth Bukusi, Dr. Maricianah Onono and Dr. Betty Njoroge through the KENSHE study (KEMRI/University of Washington) — was instrumental in establishing single-dose efficacy globally.

# Uterus, Fallopian Tubes & Ovaries

---

## The Uterus (Womb)

A hollow, pear-shaped muscular organ — the central anatomical landmark of the female internal genitalia. Before pregnancy it is ~7–8 cm long. During pregnancy it expands to the ribcage and can weigh up to 1 kg. After menopause it atrophies and shrinks back down. About 80% of uteri are **"anteverted"** (tilted forward toward the bladder); others are **"retroverted"** (tilted backward) — both are normal variations, not problems.

### Three Layers of the Uterine Wall

**Perimetrium:** Thin outer serous membrane covering the uterus.

**Myometrium:** The thick middle muscular layer — powerfully contracts during menstruation and labour. Where fibroids grow.

**Endometrium:** The inner mucosal layer with two sublayers — the permanent *basal layer* and the *functional layer* that thickens and sheds monthly.

### Four Parts of the Uterus

**Fundus:** Broad, curved top above the fallopian tube openings.

**Body (Corpus):** Main central portion where most of the organ is.

**Isthmus:** Narrowed lower neck just above the cervix.

**Cervix:** The lower cylindrical part connecting to the vagina (covered in detail on the previous page).

Held in place by broad, round and uterosacral ligaments.

### Three Key Functions

**1. Menstruation:** Each month, the endometrium thickens in preparation for potential pregnancy; sheds if no fertilisation occurs.

**2. Implantation & fetal development:** A fertilised egg implants into the nutrient-rich endometrium; the uterus becomes the fetus's home throughout pregnancy.

**3. Labour & delivery:** Myometrial contractions dilate the cervix and push the baby through the birth canal.

---

## The Fallopian Tubes — Where Fertilisation Happens

Two narrow, muscular canals (~11–12 cm long) connecting the ovaries to the uterus. The tubes are *not* directly attached to the ovaries — they have finger-like projections called **fimbriae** that reach out to sweep the egg into the tube at ovulation. The tubes are alive with movement: cilia create a current and muscular walls contract to transport egg, sperm and embryo.

### Four Regions of Each Tube

**Intramural:** The shortest, narrowest section passing through the uterine wall.

**Isthmus:** Narrow segment next to the uterus.

**Ampulla:** The longest, widest part — most common site of fertilisation. Has folded lining for increased surface area.

**Infundibulum:** The funnel-shaped open end near the ovary, fringed with fimbriae.

### Tubal Conditions

**Salpingitis/PID:** Infection (usually chlamydia/gonorrhoea) causing inflammation, often with no symptoms. Can scar and block tubes, causing infertility.

**Hydrosalpinx:** Fluid-filled blocked tube — often from prior infection; causes infertility.

**Ectopic Pregnancy:** Fertilised egg implants in the tube instead of the uterus — a **medical emergency**. Symptoms: sudden sharp one-sided pain, vaginal bleeding, shoulder tip pain, dizziness. Requires immediate care.

**Tubal factor infertility:** Accounts for up to one-third of infertility cases.

---

## The Ovaries — Hormonal Architects

Two small, oval-shaped organs (~3.5 × 2 × 1 cm in reproductive years; shrink after menopause) — the female equivalent of the testes. They have two essential roles: producing eggs and secreting hormones.

### Egg Production — A Finite Resource

You are born with all the eggs you will ever have: ~6–7 million in utero, ~1–2 million at birth, ~300,000 at puberty. Only 300–400 will ever be ovulated. The rest naturally degenerate through **atresia**. You cannot make new eggs. This is why age-related fertility decline is real — both egg quantity and quality decrease over time.

### Hormones the Ovaries Produce

**Estrogen:** Develops secondary sex characteristics, thickens the uterine lining, maintains bone density, skin health, mood.

**Progesterone:** Prepares the uterine lining for implantation; dominant in the second half of the cycle; maintains early pregnancy.

**Testosterone:** Yes — women produce testosterone in small amounts; plays roles in libido, mood and muscle mass.

**Inhibin:** Regulates FSH production in the pituitary.

⚠️ **Seek Urgent Care If:**

Sudden, severe, one-sided pelvic pain could indicate **ovarian torsion** (twisting of the ovary on its ligaments, cutting off blood supply) or a **ruptured ovarian cyst** — both are medical emergencies. Pain accompanied by fever, nausea/vomiting or faintness requires immediate attention. Heavy bleeding with pain, or bleeding combined with a positive pregnancy test, also warrant urgent evaluation.

# The Urethra & Urinary Health

*The urethra is a narrow tube (~4 cm long) connecting the urinary bladder to the outside. Its primary job is urine elimination — it is NOT part of the reproductive system. However, because of its close proximity to the vagina and anus, it is deeply connected to pelvic health.*

---

## Structure: Three Layers

**Mucosal layer (inner):** Lined with transitional epithelium near the bladder, squamous near the opening. Contains folds allowing stretching.

**Spongy layer (submucosa):** Rich in blood vessels and elastic fibres; helps seal the urethra closed when not in use.

**Muscular layer (outer):** Smooth muscle fibres plus the external sphincter (voluntary control).

## Two Sphincters Control Urination

**Internal urethral sphincter:** Involuntary — opens automatically when the bladder is full.

**External urethral sphincter:** Voluntary — you control this to hold urine or let it out. Supported by the pelvic floor muscles.

The sensation "I need to pee" is triggered by **stretch receptors in the bladder wall**, not the urethra, when the bladder is about half full.

---

## UTI — Urinary Tract Infection

A UTI is **not an STI** — it is a completely separate system. Most commonly caused by E. coli from the digestive tract entering the urethra. The short female urethra (~4 cm vs 20 cm in males) is why women are far more prone.

| Symptom | Risk Factor | Prevention |
|---------|------------|-----------|
| Pain or burning during urination (dysuria) | Wiping back to front (brings bacteria forward) | **Always wipe front to back** — single most important hygiene habit |
| Frequent urge to urinate, passing only small amounts | Holding urine for too long | Urinate when you feel the urge; don't hold it |
| Cloudy, dark or bloody urine | Sexual activity ("honeymoon cystitis" — bacteria pushed into urethra during intercourse) | **Urinate after sex** — one of the most effective prevention strategies |
| Strong-smelling urine | Menopause (low oestrogen thins urethral tissues, reducing defences) | Stay hydrated — dilutes urine and flushes bacteria |
| Pelvic pressure or incomplete emptying | Spermicides or diaphragms | Wear breathable cotton underwear; avoid tight synthetics |

🚨 **Seek Urgent Care — Signs the Infection Has Reached the Kidneys**

**Fever, chills or shaking · Nausea or vomiting · Pain in your back or side (flank pain, just below ribs) · Inability to urinate · Visibly bloody urine (red or pink)**

These indicate **pyelonephritis** (kidney infection) — a more serious condition requiring immediate treatment. A simple lower UTI often has NO fever — do not wait for fever to seek care.

## Kenya Myth-Busting: Urinary Health

| ❌ Myth | ✅ Truth |
|--------|---------|
| "Drinking less water will reduce the burning" | Burning is caused by inflammation, not water volume. Drinking less concentrates urine, making burning worse. Drinking more helps dilute and flush bacteria. |
| "If I have a UTI, I will always have a fever" | A simple lower UTI (urethritis/cystitis) often causes NO fever. Fever signals the infection has moved up to the kidneys — a more serious condition. You can be very sick with a UTI without early fever. |
| "You can stop antibiotics once the burning stops" | Dangerous. Stopping early leaves the strongest bacteria behind, creating a resistant recurrent infection. Always finish the full prescribed course. |
